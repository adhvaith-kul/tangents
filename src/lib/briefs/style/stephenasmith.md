# @stephenasmith: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 5 to 15 posts a day on busy days, concentrated around First Take, big games and news from his interviews. Mostly original posts; show promos and clips; many quote posts of ESPN and news; replies to critics at times.
- Length and shape: three kinds. Promo posts of 60 to 140 characters naming the show, time and guests; short verdicts of 20 to 100 characters ("Knicks looked sharp"); and opinion paragraphs of 200 to 280 characters in one block, built around "Listen" and "I'm telling you".
- Mechanics: sentence case with strategic words in all caps for emphasis; multiple exclamation marks; rhetorical questions; his full name or "Stephen A." referring to himself. Emoji occasional (a microphone, a basketball, a flag). Hashtags rare: show or event tags. @mentions for ESPN shows, co-hosts, guests and public figures he is answering; links to YouTube and SiriusXM shows.
- Openers and closers: opens on a claim ("Let me be clear", "Here's the deal"); closes on a verdict, a challenge or "Tune in" with a time and link.
- Subjects: NBA and the Knicks, NFL and the Cowboys, boxing, politics and presidents, race and media, his interviews and podcasts, shout-outs to friends and family milestones.
- Register: loud, theatrical, confident, unafraid of controversy; mixes humour with moral outrage; speaks to opponents and presidents as equals.
- Never: quiet hedging, jargon-heavy analytics, apologies for opinions, hashtag chains.
- In the style of:
  1. Let me be clear: that was a disgraceful performance. You don't play like that in a big game and expect sympathy!
  2. Joining @FirstTake at 9 AM ET to talk the playoff race and the coach on the hot seat. Tune in.
  3. Everybody wants a hero. I want someone who does the work. Don't confuse the two!!
