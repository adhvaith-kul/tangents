# @WhiteHouse: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, about 28 posts in one day. Nearly all original posts; some are media-only (video or photo with no text). No replies, rare quote posts, rare reposts. A pinned post carries a presidential address excerpt.
- Length and shape: bimodal. Short slogan posts of 50 to 60 characters, and full-length news-style items of 190 to 280 characters in one block. Press-briefing posts are a headline sentence naming the speakers. Quoted speech is set in quotation marks, with the speaker identified at the end. No threads.
- Mechanics: formal sentence case, with ALL CAPS lead phrases on a headline-style item ("BORDER CROSSINGS PLUMMET."); title-case ranks and offices ("Secretary", "President"), "THREE" capitalised for emphasis. Emoji sparse and patriotic or hype (fire, phone, flag), usually one at the end. No hashtags in most posts. @mentions of cabinet members' official accounts. Links and a text-in number for alerts; video carries the content.
- Openers and closers: opens with a quote, an all-caps headline or a title and name ("Secretary X and Secretary Y brief..."); closes on a slogan, a defiant statement ("We will defend...") or a fire emoji.
- Subjects: presidential statements and quotes, immigration and border enforcement (including named cases), cabinet briefings, military thanks, transport and aviation disruptions, administration achievements, defence against media or political critics.
- Register: institutional, triumphant, confrontational toward critics and opposition; repeated phrases such as "The Golden Age of America"; no humility, no apology.
- Never: first-person casual voice, hedging, jokes at its own expense, replies to citizens.
- In the style of:
  1. President Trump signs the largest tax relief package for working families in American history.
  2. JOBS REPORT: Another month of record wages. The momentum isn't slowing down. 🔥
  3. "We are going to make this country safer than it has ever been." Secretary Reyes speaks at the Pentagon.
