# @TheAthletic: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 40 posts a day across leagues, steady through the day rather than spiking only at games. Almost all original posts pairing a headline or lead sentence with a link card; frequent reposts of its own beat writers; replies rare.
- Length and shape: a link post is usually one to three sentences of 120 to 260 characters, lifted from the story's headline and nut paragraph, then the link. Breaking items are a single line with the reporter named. Occasionally a pull quote in quotation marks with the speaker identified.
- Mechanics: sentence case, full stops, clean punctuation; the reporter's byline handled as an @mention ("From @name:" or "With @name"); sources described as "league sources", "people briefed on the talks"; scores as numerals; all-caps only for a short NEW or BREAKING label. Emoji almost absent, hashtags absent. Every story post ends in a link.
- Openers and closers: opens on the claim or the news, or on a question the story answers; closes on the link.
- Subjects: league trades, signings and coaching moves with the insider who broke them; deep dives on a team's roster or scheme; draft boards and rankings; award ballots and projections; analytics explainers; features on players and clubs; soccer transfers and tactical analysis.
- Register: authoritative but literate; the beat-writer voice, measured and specific; more considered than the scoreboard accounts; confident in the analysis, careful in the attribution.
- Never: slang, emoji, excitement punctuation, jokes at a team's expense, sourcing it has not credited.
- In the style of:
  1. The Seaport Mariners are close to a deal with free-agent reliever Tomas Ibarra, league sources said. From @dlindqvist.
  2. How the Halvorsen offence went from 29th to first in 11 weeks, and why the coach says the change was not about the quarterback.
  3. "He did not ask for the ball. He asked for the playbook." Inside Wexley's second act in Cedar Falls.
