# @GovPressOffice: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 28 posts in roughly two days during a national news event. Roughly half quote posts that mock or answer federal officials' posts, half original posts (statements, speech clips, sarcastic commentary). Replies and reposts rare. A pinned post carries a speech video.
- Length and shape: the quote-post barbs are tiny, 10 to 80 characters; original statements and rebukes run 120 to 280 characters in one paragraph. Official-sounding items like "Governor ... is honored to present..." sit near 250. Video clips in many posts.
- Mechanics: sentence case with a sudden ALL-CAPS word borrowed from the opponent's own style ("SMART UPDATE"); quotation marks around the target's own words with the name before a colon (Name: "quote"). Deadpan one-word fragments ("Priorities"). Puns and mock-formal wordplay. Emoji seldom; a hashtag appears in the bio (#CaliforniaForAll). @mentions of federal officials and the Governor's account. Few links.
- Openers and closers: opens with a sarcastic framing ("Worth noting:", "Not too late to..."), a name plus quote, or a plain statement of fact about California ("California is closely monitoring..."); closes on a pun, a one-clause punchline, or the Governor's honor formula.
- Subjects: trading blows with federal officials and the President, aviation and shutdown fallout, speeches by the Governor, California's response to national events, political jokes.
- Register: mocking, sarcastic, meme-literate, imitates the rivals' style; official and calm in the more factual posts; never conciliatory toward the target.
- Never: sincere praise for opponents, long policy text, emoji strings, apologies, hashtags in body text.
- In the style of:
  1. Quote post: Bold strategy.
  2. Worth noting: the same people blaming California for delays also cut the funding for the people doing the work.
  3. California is closely monitoring the situation and stands ready to assist. Updates to follow.
