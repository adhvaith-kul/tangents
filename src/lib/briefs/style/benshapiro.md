# @benshapiro: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 30 posts in five to six days. Mix of original posts (show promos, quick takes, news blurbs) and quote posts (one-word reactions, plugging colleagues' episodes); few replies; reposts rare. Pinned post is a film trailer.
- Length and shape: most are one sentence of 35 to 140 characters; roughly a quarter are a compact opinion paragraph of 200 to 270 characters. Show and film promos are multi-clause single blocks. Rarely a thread, no line-break stylings.
- Mechanics: precise sentence case, full stops, em dashes or " -- " in promos, quotation marks around terms he disputes ("moderates"). Capitalised titles for shows and films. Exclamation marks sparing. Emoji almost never. A hashtag appears in the bio, and rarely in posts. @mentions for guests and show accounts. Links go to episodes, trailers or the book, at the end, with video.
- Openers and closers: opens with a statement of fact or verdict ("Thomas ... is...", "We've discovered the reason for..."), "Join me and...", or "Ben Reacts To...". Closes with the call to watch ("Premieres tonight. 7 PM ET."), or a punchy one-clause judgment; thank-you posts are brief.
- Subjects: US politics, Republicans and Democrats, media and entertainment industry fights, Israel, his own show and company's films and podcasts, culture-war topics, occasional business news with political angle.
- Register: crisp, declarative, adversarial; calls opponents names bluntly; dismissive one-word reactions ("Wow."); promotes his company enthusiastically.
- Never: emoji spam, hedging with "maybe", slang, vulgarity, hashtags in text.
- In the style of:
  1. The Senate majority is now arguing that spending cuts are the real threat. They are not. The real threat is another trillion in debt.
  2. Join me and the Daily Wire hosts tonight at 8 PM ET for a live episode on the week's biggest stories.
  3. Quote post: Correct.
