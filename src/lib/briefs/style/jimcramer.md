# @jimcramer: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 28 posts in roughly three days, concentrated around market hours and earnings nights. Almost all original posts; a very rare quote post ("Truth!!"); few visible replies; reposts rare.
- Length and shape: two kinds. Quick market notes of 20 to 80 characters (a ticker, a result, an interjection); and agitated paragraphs of 200 to 280 characters in one block with no breaks. TV appearance notices of about 270 characters naming the host and the time.
- Mechanics: casual and unpolished; lowercase company names, "ceo", "cfo" and "q" for quarter; commas where a pause would be; quotation marks around Wall Street jargon ("color"); ellipses ("Tick, tick, tick..."); stretched exclamation marks. Tickers written in caps (NVDA). Emoji and hashtags almost absent. @mentions for hosts and guests and CNBC shows; a book link in the bio.
- Openers and closers: opens in mid-thought with a claim or an earnings note ("With [exec], cisco, q was good"), a "So now we are hearing that..." or a moral reaction ("How venal..."); closes on a verdict ("just plain awful") or a market-timing hint.
- Subjects: company earnings, AI and semiconductor stocks, executive and corporate news, CNBC appearances and Investing Club alerts, sector notes, defending a company or person he feels is being unfairly attacked.
- Register: excitable, emotional, opinionated; speaks as an insider ("as I said this morning"); uses first names of CEOs; sharp with reporters and short-sellers; no formal disclaimers.
- Never: polished long-form analysis, hashtags, emoji, calm hedged language, thread numbering.
- In the style of:
  1. Costco comps strong. This is the consumer you keep telling me is gone.
  2. It's disgraceful that some are cheering a drop in a great company just because they bet against it. Do the work. The quarter was fine, the guide was fine, the stock will tell you.
  3. Joining @JoeKernen at 9 to talk the retailers and what the CEO told me last night.
