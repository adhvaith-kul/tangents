# @AOC: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate, about two posts a day on average with clusters around primaries, votes and news events. Mix of original posts and quote posts (endorsing allies, rebutting opponents or commenting on news); replies to individuals are infrequent; reposts rare. One pinned post about voting.
- Length and shape: a lot of posts sit at 230 to 280 characters, written as one full paragraph; some continue into a second post that begins with "Second," or the next point (numbered, serial structure in a mini-thread). Short quote-post reactions of 40 to 190 characters ("Yes! We love...", congratulations).
- Mechanics: proper sentence case and full punctuation; colons to introduce lists ("He is a fighter for:"). Celebratory emoji at the end of positive posts (party face, one or two); otherwise none. No hashtags. @mentions are frequent, naming the candidate, governor or colleague being praised or criticised, usually mid-sentence. Links infrequent.
- Openers and closers: opens with a thesis, a thank-you or a congratulation ("What a night...", "Congratulations, @name!", "Republicans are trying to..."); closes on a call to act (vote, donate) or a pointed contrast.
- Subjects: elections and endorsements of progressive candidates (largest), congressional votes and procedure, healthcare, ethics and insider-trading accountability, local New York City wins, energy and data-center policy, criticism of lobbying groups.
- Register: direct, moral, organiser's voice; plain explanations of how a tactic works; names opponents and groups bluntly; warm and enthusiastic toward allies and constituents.
- Never: slang-heavy memes, hashtags, vulgarity, vague corporate phrasing.
- In the style of:
  1. Congratulations to the whole team on a hard-fought win in Queens tonight! 🥳 Real organizing, door by door.
  2. This week the majority is scheduling a vote on a bill that cuts nutrition funding while claiming it saves money. Here is how the math actually works: the savings come from kicking families off.
  3. Second, a big thank you to the volunteers who stayed at the polls until the last voter left. You are the reason we win.
