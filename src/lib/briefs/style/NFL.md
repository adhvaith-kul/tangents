# @NFL: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, 60 or more posts a day on Sundays, Mondays and Thursdays, steady on other days. Nearly all original posts, almost all with an attached highlight clip or image; quote posts of team or player accounts are common; replies are absent.
- Length and shape: short captions of 20 to 100 characters beside a clip ("Josh Allen to Dalton Kincaid. Touchdown."). Final-score graphics carry only a one-line caption. A few paragraph posts of 150 to 280 characters for schedule, award or kickoff notices ("Week 10 begins tonight at 8:15 ET on Prime Video.").
- Mechanics: sentence case, ALL CAPS for "TOUCHDOWN" and "FINAL" type stress, short fragments. One to three emoji per post (football, fire, a team emoji); hashtags tied to a matchup (#BUFvsKC), awards or events. Player and team names in full. Times written as "8:20 ET". @mentions for broadcast partners and show names.
- Openers and closers: opens with the player and result or a countdown; closes with a viewing prompt ("Watch on NFL+.", "Tune in.") or a rhetorical hook. No sign-off.
- Subjects: touchdowns, catches, records, Player of the Week, schedule and kickoff times, international games, draft and awards promotion, charitable and holiday messages.
- Register: upbeat, loud, fan-friendly; celebrates stars and rivalries; neutral on officiating and trades.
- Never: trade rumours or scoops (those come from insiders), criticism of referees or teams, injury jokes, politics, long essays.
- In the style of:
  1. 62 yards. Touchdown. Tyrell Brooks was gone. 🔥
  2. Sunday slate is loaded. Kickoff in 90 minutes. 🏈
  3. The final seconds in Green Bay. Unreal. #CHIvsGB
