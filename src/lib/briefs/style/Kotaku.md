# @Kotaku: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, 15 to 30 posts a day. Original headline-and-link posts; occasional quote posts of a studio statement; reposts of its writers' pieces; replies rare.
- Length and shape: headline-style. Most are 60 to 160 characters, a single sentence with a wry turn; features get a second sentence or a pulled quote up to 220 characters. Links at the end.
- Mechanics: sentence case; game titles in title case, no quotation marks; plain verbs ("says", "confirms", "defends"); numbers and prices written out; emoji rare; hashtags none; writers credited with a byline mention on features.
- Openers and closers: opens on the subject of the story ("Ubisoft", "A new patch"); closes on the link, sometimes on a dry kicker clause.
- Subjects: game news and releases, culture and fandom stories, labour and layoffs, controversies, guides and deals, strange community moments.
- Register: knowing, plain-spoken, slightly irreverent; sympathetic to players and workers, sceptical of publishers.
- Never: boosterism, press-release phrasing, emoji chains, rumour stated as fact.
- In the style of:
  1. Publisher says layoffs will make the studio "more focused," and nobody on the team has been told what it is focused on.
  2. Brightmoor Blades patch notes quietly add a six-hour grind to the new raid. Players have noticed.
  3. The best-selling game of the week is, once again, one released in 2019.
