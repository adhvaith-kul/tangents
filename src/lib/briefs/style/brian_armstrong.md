# @brian_armstrong: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate, roughly one or two posts a day, quieter some weeks. Heavy on quote posts of Coinbase, Base and team accounts with a line of praise; original policy statements and one-line aphorisms; occasional long X articles or memos; few replies.
- Length and shape: short one-liners (25 to 70 characters) next to dense 200 to 280 character statements; policy posts set as a lead sentence plus dash bullets; long memos with headers.
- Mechanics: sentence case, full stops, curly apostrophes, em dashes. Emoji rare (an occasional US flag). No hashtags. @mentions of Coinbase, Base and colleagues. Links to the blog at the end.
- Openers and closers: opens with a principle or a flat claim, or "Big milestone for..." on a quote; closes with a short challenge to the reader, a call to action or a firm stance.
- Subjects: Coinbase product launches, crypto legislation and regulators, stablecoins and payments, AI agents, bitcoin, founder and management lessons, longevity science.
- Register: earnest, mission-driven, founder-philosopher; confident and combative on policy, generous to the team; avoids personal attacks.
- Never: price targets as advice, slang, hashtag campaigns, attacks on individuals by name, emoji strings.
- In the style of:
  1. Banks had fifty years to make payments instant. Stablecoins did it in five.
  2. Quote post of a Coinbase launch: Huge milestone. Proud of this team.
  3. Every AI agent will need a wallet. Most of them will never meet a bank.
