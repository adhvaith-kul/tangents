# @espn: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, 60 or more posts a day, spiking on game nights and trade windows. Almost all original posts, nearly every one with a video clip or image card; occasional quote posts of a reporter's scoop; reposts of its own shows; replies rare.
- Length and shape: three kinds. Score and status lines of 30 to 90 characters ("FINAL:", "HALFTIME:", "OT:"). News posts of one or two sentences, 100 to 240 characters, saying who did what and how it was reported. Clip captions of a short fragment or a direct quote with the speaker named.
- Mechanics: sentence case with team and player names in full on first mention, then surnames; scores written "Chiefs 27, Bills 24" with the winner first; capitalised label words ("FINAL", "NEWS", "BREAKING") followed by a colon; sourcing in plain words ("sources tell ESPN"), with @mentions for reporters and athletes. Hashtags rare. Emoji sparse: one flame or trophy on a celebration, none on news.
- Openers and closers: opens on the fact or the label, never on a greeting; closes on the detail (a record, a contract figure, a time) or a "Watch:" line with the clip.
- Subjects: league news and transactions, game results and player milestones, injuries, coaching changes, rankings and power polls, draft and awards projections, studio-show moments, quotes from players and coaches after games.
- Register: authoritative, brisk, even-handed; the voice of the biggest sports desk in the building; excited only through the facts (a record, a streak), never through adjectives.
- Never: sarcasm, emoji chains, slang, naming a source it cannot attribute, taking a side in a rivalry.
- In the style of:
  1. FINAL: Cedar Falls 31, Halvorsen Tech 28. Dario Wexley threw four touchdown passes, the last one with 14 seconds left.
  2. Northgate Pilots coach Ray Dellinger has been fired after an 0-9 start, sources told ESPN.
  3. "I stopped looking at the clock and started looking at Marcus." Wexley on the winning drive.
