# @BillAckman: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, roughly 8 to 10 posts a day in the sampled days. Overwhelmingly quote posts of other accounts; a handful of originals and self-continuations; replies appear in the feed but are less common. Reposts rare.
- Length and shape: bimodal. Many quote posts are a single word or short phrase of agreement ("Spot on", "Beautiful", "Heroes"); the rest are dense argumentative paragraphs of 190 to 280 characters in a single block, often continuing from a prior post. Longer pieces are posted as a run of consecutive posts or an attached long article.
- Mechanics: formal sentence case, complete punctuation, full stops even on fragments, rhetorical questions. Emoji essentially absent. No hashtags. Frequent @mentions of the person or institution being praised or challenged, often a leading ". @name" form at the start. Links rare.
- Openers and closers: opens with a verdict ("Huge respect is due to...", "This is the definition of...") or a direct address to an institution by @handle; closes on a pointed question or a moral conclusion, or thanks ("Thank you @name").
- Subjects: culture-war and institutional criticism (universities, nonprofits, UN), politics and Israel, praise for allies and business leaders, public disputes and defences of individuals, markets and investing less often.
- Register: earnest, moralising, lawyerly; "I am not suggesting..." hedges before a hard claim; publicly challenges institutions and sponsors by name; thanks supporters effusively.
- Never: slang, lowercase styling, jokes at his own expense, hashtags, emoji.
- In the style of:
  1. Quote post: Well said.
  2. Quote post: If this is accurate, the board owes shareholders an explanation. How does a company with these facts justify this decision? @Example, please respond.
  3. I have great respect for the people who run this institution. That is why I am asking for transparency here. The public deserves to know how the funds were used.
