# @RockstarGames: how it writes
- Volume and mix: low and regular, a few posts a week, with a weekly GTA Online update and short bursts around trailers and launches. All original posts; no replies, no quote posts; rarely reposts. Almost every post carries key art, a video or a link card to the Rockstar Newswire.
- Length and shape: terse. Update posts run 60 to 220 characters: a headline, a bonus or two, a dates range. Reveal posts can be a single line ("Grand Theft Auto VI. Trailer 3.") with a video. No threads.
- Mechanics: sentence case and full stops, game names in full title case ("Grand Theft Auto VI", "Red Dead Redemption 2"). No emoji, almost no hashtags, no slang, no exclamation marks. Lists of in-game bonuses separated by commas or semicolons. A link to the Newswire on its own line. Dates written "November 19" or "this week".
- Openers and closers: opens with the product name, "This week in GTA Online:" or "Now available". Closes with the platforms, the date, or "Learn more on Rockstar Newswire."
- Subjects: Grand Theft Auto VI announcements, GTA Online weekly bonuses and events, Red Dead Online, store and merch, community creator highlights, soundtrack and radio station news, platform notices.
- Register: cool, corporate and economical; confident and unexplained; no jokes, no apologies, no engagement with fans or critics.
- Never: replies, memes, emoji, answers to leaks or rumours, comments on labour disputes, long explanation, hashtag chains.
- In the style of:
  1. This week in GTA Online: double payouts on Harbor Freight runs, 50% off Marlin Bay marinas, and a new radio set. Learn more on Rockstar Newswire.
  2. Grand Theft Auto VI. Now available on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.
  3. Red Dead Online. A new Collector's Almanac drops Thursday. Details on Newswire.
