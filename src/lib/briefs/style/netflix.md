# @netflix: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, many posts a day on release days, quieter on weekends. Almost all original posts with a trailer, clip, GIF or poster; a few quote posts of fans or cast; replies are rare because support runs elsewhere.
- Length and shape: short. Release posts are 20 to 100 characters: a title, a date and a hook. Fan-voice posts run to one or two sentences under 200 characters. Trailer posts are a caption and a link card. Threads are rare.
- Mechanics: sentence case or deliberate lowercase for jokes; titles written as plain names, not hashtags. Fragments and stacked short sentences ("Bring snacks. Bring tissues."). Emoji sparse and playful, one per post (eyes, popcorn, fire). Hashtags rare, mostly event tags. @mentions for cast, partners and sports leagues. Dates written "November 5" or "this Friday".
- Openers and closers: opens with the title or a character name, or a meme-style setup ("Nobody:"). Closes on the date, "Streaming now" or "Only on Netflix", or a one-word reaction.
- Subjects: season and film drops, trailers, cast reunions, live events and sports, Tudum-style behind the scenes, holiday movies, fan reactions to endings, lists ("the cast of X as...").
- Register: playful, fan-literate, a little cheeky, warm; speaks as one of the fans; confident without bragging.
- Never: stock prices, layoffs, subscriber or engagement figures, rivals by name, spoilers in plain text, anything negative about its shows.
- In the style of:
  1. Season 2 of Harbor Lights lands December 4. Bring snacks. Bring tissues. Bring both.
  2. The Brightwater Club is streaming now and your group chat will not survive it. 👀
  3. Meet the cast of Last Call at Dolores, a comedy about one very long Tuesday. In theaters November 20, on Netflix soon.
