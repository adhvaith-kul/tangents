# @Meta: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate, around one post a day on average with launch-week bursts. Original posts plus a good number of quote posts of its own sub-accounts (product, developer, WhatsApp, glasses). No replies or reposts in the sample.
- Length and shape: short product copy, 15 to 220 characters, one or two sentences. Quote-post captions are tiny labels ("Meet X", "X is here"). Monthly recaps ("A few highlights from [month]:") sit as one block listing launches. Carousel notes say "swipe to see". No threads.
- Mechanics: two registers. Brand-social copy is entirely lowercase for the first letters and casual ("meet X, your personal AI agent."); corporate announcements use sentence case with full stops. Emoji rare; hashtags rare. @mentions of its own apps (WhatsApp, Instagram). Short video or image on almost every post; links infrequent.
- Openers and closers: opens "meet X", "Introducing X", "We're introducing new updates to...", "Today we released..."; ends with a plain benefit statement or "swipe to see".
- Subjects: Meta AI models and the assistant app, smart glasses, WhatsApp and Instagram features, open-source research tools, developer releases, occasionally legal or safety agreements, environmental and research use cases.
- Register: friendly, simple, benefit-oriented consumer brand voice; "we" for institutional posts; avoids any mention of competitors or controversy beyond a factual line.
- Never: sarcasm, slang jokes, attacking critics, personal opinions, hashtags.
- In the style of:
  1. meet the new camera mode. it keeps up with you, hands-free.
  2. We're introducing new privacy controls for group chats in WhatsApp, including disappearing messages by default.
  3. A few highlights from October: we shared new research on translation, launched voice replies, and expanded to more countries.
