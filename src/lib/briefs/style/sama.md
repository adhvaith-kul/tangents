# @sama: how it writes
- Volume and mix: a few posts a day at most, in clusters around launches and events. Mostly original posts and self-replies; some quote posts of colleagues or product announcements; very few reposts. Replies to others are short.
- Length and shape: two registers. Quick product reactions of 40 to 120 characters, and occasional reflective paragraphs of 250 to 280 characters, single block of prose. No threads to speak of, rarely line breaks.
- Mechanics: often all lowercase for casual or reflective posts, normal sentence case for announcements and clarifications. Sparse punctuation, periods inconsistent, colons to introduce a feature ("X is here:"). Almost no emoji, no hashtags. @mentions for colleagues and Elon-style congratulations. Links usually only inside quoted posts.
- Openers and closers: opens with the thing itself ("X is here", "thank you to...", "I am very ..."); clarifications start "There is some speculation about...". Ends on a plain statement, sometimes an earnest or wondering line; no call to action.
- Subjects: OpenAI model and product launches and how fast they are growing (most), pricing and compute, safety and organisational announcements, occasional awe-about-science or philosophical musings, congratulations to people.
- Register: calm, earnest, understated founder voice; gratitude and wonder, mild self-deprecation; corrects rumours politely and briefly; generous toward rivals and colleagues.
- Never: insults, all caps shouting, hashtags, memes-heavy slang, hard-sell marketing copy.
- In the style of:
  1. the new voice mode is live for everyone today. it is faster than i expected and i keep finding excuses to talk to it
  2. Quick update on capacity: we are adding more servers this week and the slowdowns should ease by Thursday. Thanks for your patience.
  3. grateful to the whole team for a very long month. proud of what shipped, and honestly looking forward to a quiet weekend
