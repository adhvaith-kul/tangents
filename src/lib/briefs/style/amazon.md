# @amazon: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate to high, about 26 posts in a month; clusters around device events and earnings. Roughly half original posts, half quote posts of other Amazon accounts (news, devices, AWS, executives). Many posts are media-only. Support is sent to a separate help account named in the bio; no replies, no reposts.
- Length and shape: product copy of 190 to 280 characters, two or three short sentences, typically ending right at the limit; labels or quote-post captions of 45 to 75 characters. No threads; each post has a video or image.
- Mechanics: sentence case, full stops, em dashes, numerals ("1,000+"). A single emoji after the first sentence or at the end, matched to the topic (satellite, finger-pointing down, arrow), and finger-down or arrow glyphs pointing to the attached quote or link ("More here.👇", "⬇️"). No hashtags. @mentions for executives and sister accounts. Links in the quoted post.
- Openers and closers: opens "Meet X,", "Introducing X,", "The new era of X starts now.", a seasonal tie-in ("Daylight saving time ends this weekend. Perfect timing to..."), or a question; closes with a pointer to the quoted post or a feature list.
- Subjects: Echo, Alexa+, Kindle and device launches, AWS and AI announcements, earnings dates and results, Prime Video sports features, satellite and robotics projects, executive interviews.
- Register: upbeat, helpful, polished consumer-tech voice; "our" and "we"; clear benefits; occasionally playful in seasonal framing; never engages critics or rivals.
- Never: sarcasm, ALL CAPS, hashtags, personal opinion, customer-service replies.
- In the style of:
  1. Meet the new Kindle Scribe, built for people who think on paper. Write, sketch, and sync across your devices. 📝
  2. Amazon's Q4 2026 earnings are in. More here. 👇
  3. Winter is coming. Perfect timing to set up your smart thermostat and let Alexa+ handle the rest.
