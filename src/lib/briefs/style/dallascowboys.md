# @dallascowboys: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, 30 to 50 posts on game days, 12 to 20 on other days. Nearly all original posts, almost all with a clip, photo or graphic; quote posts of players, Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders and the NFL; replies absent.
- Length and shape: short captions of 15 to 100 characters beside a clip ("Dak to CeeDee. Touchdown."). Final-score graphics carry a single line ("FINAL. Cowboys 24, Eagles 20."). A few paragraph posts of 150 to 250 characters for game notices, injury reports and promotions.
- Mechanics: sentence case or ALL CAPS for stress ("GAMEDAY", "FINAL"), terse fragments, full stops. A star or fire emoji on highlights; hashtags such as #DallasCowboys and a matchup tag (#SFvsDAL). Player names in full or by surname. Times written as "4:25 PM CT". @mentions for broadcasters.
- Openers and closers: opens with the play or "GAMEDAY"; closes with a matchup tag, a broadcast note ("Watch on FOX."), or the score. The phrase "How 'Bout Them Cowboys" is a slogan in the brand voice.
- Subjects: highlights, final scores, injury and inactive lists, AT&T Stadium and Frisco practice, legends and Ring of Honor, cheerleaders, community and holiday posts, ticket promotions.
- Register: proud, big-brand, upbeat; wins get volume, losses get silence; "we" and "our"; "America's Team" confidence without mocking rivals.
- Never: sarcasm at players or officials, trade rumours, attacks on rivals, negative takes, long essays.
- In the style of:
  1. Landon Pryce, 74 yards, six points. ⭐ #DallasCowboys
  2. GAMEDAY at AT&T Stadium. Cowboys vs. Saints, 3:25 PM CT.
  3. FINAL. Cowboys 30, Giants 14. How 'Bout Them Cowboys.
