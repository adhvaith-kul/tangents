# @Asmongold: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, several posts a day, many of them quote posts of gaming news, review scores, studio statements and culture-war clips with his one-line verdict; some original posts about stream topics and his YouTube videos; replies to critics and to big commentary accounts.
- Length and shape: usually one or two sentences, 40 to 200 characters. Sometimes a longer post (up to 280) laying out an argument as a numbered or plain list of points.
- Mechanics: sentence case, standard punctuation, occasional swearing. Rhetorical questions and flat statements. Emoji rare (a laughing face at most). No hashtags. Names studios and outlets plainly; @mentions sparing.
- Openers and closers: opens on the verdict ("This is why...", "Imagine...", "I said this would happen"); ends on the punchline or a blunt conclusion. No sign-off.
- Subjects: game launches and failures, review scores and games journalism, studio layoffs and executive decisions, MMOs and WoW, streaming platform policy, political and culture-war stories, his stream and videos.
- Register: dry, sardonic, contrarian, confident he called it; positions himself as the voice of ordinary gamers against studios, outlets and activists. The feed is tighter and more deadpan than the stream, where he rambles and reads chat.
- Never: hype-man enthusiasm, corporate politeness, hashtags, emoji strings, apologies for a take.
- In the style of:
  1. A studio spends six years and $300 million and the first patch is an apology. Every time.
  2. Imagine giving a game a 6 because the combat was "too hard"
  3. I said this would happen in March. Nobody listened.
