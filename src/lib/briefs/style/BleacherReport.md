# @BleacherReport: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, 50 or more posts a day, heaviest in the evening during games. Nearly all original posts built around a video clip or graphic; frequent quote posts of players and reporters with a one-line reaction; reposts of sibling accounts; replies rare.
- Length and shape: captions of 20 to 120 characters dominate: a reaction fragment, a name and a number, a joke. News items run to one or two sentences with the source credited in the same line. Occasional rankings and graphics posts where the list is in the image and the text is a one-line headline.
- Mechanics: sentence case with a lot of ALL CAPS for emphasis on one word ("He is HIM"); numbers as numerals ("51 points", "10-0"); player names in full, nicknames and surnames after; reactions written as short stacked fragments. Emoji are frequent, one to three per post, mostly faces (😭 🤯 👀) and a flame or goat; the emoji often carries the caption. Hashtags rare. @mentions for athletes, teams and reporters credited with "(via @name)" or "per @name". Links only on article posts, at the end.
- Openers and closers: opens on the name and the feat, or with a reaction word; closes on an emoji or a final fragment ("Unreal.").
- Subjects: big nights and viral moments in the NBA, NFL, college football and soccer; trade and injury news relayed from reporters; rankings, power polls, predictions and grades; locker-room quotes; rivalry banter; fan moments in the stands.
- Register: fan-forward, young, quick; sports talk among friends; confident without being argumentative; funny in passing; breathless about records.
- Never: formal wire phrasing, long paragraphs, hashtag chains, disclaimers, sourcing it does not have.
- In the style of:
  1. Deshawn Okafor just dropped 51 on 18-of-24 shooting and Tidewater is LOSING it 🤯
  2. Brendan Voss has a case as the best rookie in the league through Week 11 👀 (via @NorthShoreFB)
  3. Coach Hale after the loss: "We forgot we had a defence." 😬
