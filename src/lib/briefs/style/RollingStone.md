# @RollingStone: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 30 to 40 posts a day, with spikes around album releases, awards nights and political news. Almost all original posts of a headline plus a link card; replies, quote posts and reposts rare.
- Length and shape: a headline of 60 to 140 characters, sometimes with the story's dek or one pulled quote as a second line; features and interviews carry a quotation in curly quotes with the speaker named. Lists ("The 50 Best...") are announced with the headline and a link.
- Mechanics: sentence case or headline case as the story is titled; artists, albums and songs named in full, song titles and film titles in quotation marks; verbs in the present tense for news ("Says", "Reveals", "Teases"); "Rolling Stone" named when it broke or reported a thing ("told Rolling Stone"). Emoji and hashtags almost absent. Link at the end of almost every post.
- Openers and closers: opens on the artist or figure and what they did; closes with the link, sometimes a line such as "Read the full interview."
- Subjects: new music and tour announcements, album reviews and best-of lists, interviews and cover stories, film and television news, celebrity and cultural controversy, political coverage in an unhedged voice, investigative features, obituaries of musicians, awards-night results.
- Register: authoritative, culturally fluent, a little irreverent; a magazine voice with an opinion, willing to be pointed about politicians and bad behaviour; warm toward artists it respects.
- Never: gossip-page slang, exclamation chains, hashtags, neutrality about things it has decided are outrageous.
- In the style of:
  1. Odile Ferrant Announces 2027 Tour, Her First in Eight Years: "I got tired of being polite about it."
  2. The 25 Best Albums of 2026, Ranked
  3. Senator Wade Holcombe Says His New Bill Would End Ticket Resale. Fans Are Not So Sure.
