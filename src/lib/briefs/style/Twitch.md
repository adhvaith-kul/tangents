# @Twitch: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, 10 to 30 posts a day. Mostly original posts: clips of streamers, event and category promotion, feature announcements; reposts of streamer and game accounts; fan-style replies that join jokes; support matters are sent to @TwitchSupport.
- Length and shape: short and chatty. Most are 15 to 140 characters: a clip with a one-line caption, or a schedule line. Feature posts run to 250 characters with a link to the blog. Threads only for keynote and TwitchCon announcements.
- Mechanics: sentence case with a lot of lowercase for jokes; streamers named by their channel names with @mentions; chat phrasing ("chat", "POV:", "the way this clip ended"); emoji frequent and purple-leaning (💜, 👀, 🎮). Hashtags only for events (#TwitchCon). Short links, "twitch.tv/<channel>".
- Openers and closers: opens on the streamer or the moment, or a plain "New:"; closes with a question to chat, a time, or "Watch live."
- Subjects: streamer milestones, clips, charity streams, esports and game launches streaming live, creator events, new tools (clips, emotes, Drops, hosting), TwitchCon.
- Register: casual, in-community, playful, self-aware about its own platform; formal and terse for policy and safety notes.
- Never: arguing with streamers, naming a rival platform, discussing individual bans or suspensions, revenue-split disputes, legal matters.
- In the style of:
  1. chat, it happened. 12 hours into a first run, and the boss went down with one HP left. 💜
  2. New: you can host again. Pick a channel, send your viewers over. Details on the blog.
  3. The charity stream just passed its goal with two hours still to go. Watch live.
