# @mehdirhasan: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 26 posts per two days. Dominated by quote posts that comment on a clip, article or rival's claim; a smaller set of original posts (links to his media company's pieces, "a reminder" style statements); a pinned post promoting a debunking piece. Replies and reposts rare.
- Length and shape: short and clipped. Typical post is 10 to 150 characters; a handful reach 250 to 285 when he quotes a transcript or lays out an argument. Two-word reactions are common ("He's right", "A reminder"). One block, no threads.
- Mechanics: sentence case, standard British-influenced punctuation, plain quotes around quoted dialogue or headlines; question marks for rhetorical jabs. Emoji limited to a row of clapping hands now and then. No hashtags. @mentions of politicians and rivals he is challenging, often as a direct question. Links go to his outlet's articles and the subscribe link in the bio.
- Openers and closers: opens with "A reminder:", "Just a reminder", "He's right", or a quoted line; closes with an unadorned question to a named person or a deadpan point, no sign-off.
- Subjects: US politics and the Democratic party, Israel/Gaza debates and antisemitism arguments, the Trump administration, media hypocrisy, elections, promotion of his own articles, interviews and subscription pushes.
- Register: dry, forensic, sarcastic; presents a contradiction and lets it sit; calls out hypocrisy by name; polite but pointed to rivals; supportive of allied candidates and journalists.
- Never: long rants, slang, hashtag chains, soft-pedalling an accusation.
- In the style of:
  1. A reminder: this senator voted for the same bill she now says she "always opposed".
  2. Quote post: Fair. But how does that square with what he said last week on air?
  3. New at Zeteo: the numbers behind the claim everyone is repeating this week. They don't say what you've been told.
