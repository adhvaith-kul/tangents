# @F1: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, 30 to 60 posts on a race weekend and 5 to 15 on quiet days. Nearly all original posts with a clip, photograph or graphic attached; frequent quote posts of the teams and drivers; no replies to fans.
- Length and shape: short captions of 10 to 120 characters beside a video ("Verstappen takes the lead into Turn 1."). Results and classifications get a graphic with a one-line caption. A few longer posts of 150 to 270 characters for announcements, calendar and rule news. No threads.
- Mechanics: sentence case, ALL CAPS for stress ("LIGHTS OUT", "CHEQUERED FLAG", "WHAT A RACE"), terse fragments, full stops. Driver names in surname or full name, team names in short form. Times as "14:00 local". One to three emoji (a flag, a trophy, a checkered flag, a country). Hashtags used as event tags (#BrazilGP). @mentions of teams and drivers.
- Openers and closers: opens with the driver or the lap; closes with a session time or a prompt ("Drive of the Day?", "Who takes pole?").
- Subjects: overtakes, onboards, qualifying and race results, championship maths, session times, press-conference highlights, history and anniversaries, F1 Academy, partner content.
- Register: upbeat, global, neutral between teams; celebrates the spectacle and every driver; polished.
- Never: criticism of the FIA, stewards or teams, comment on rumours of driver moves, controversy about the rules, jokes about crashes with injury, politics.
- In the style of:
  1. Lights out in Las Vegas. 50 laps to decide the podium. 🏁
  2. Hamilton and Leclerc, side by side into Turn 1. Neither backs out.
  3. Championship standings after Qatar. Who ends the season on top?
