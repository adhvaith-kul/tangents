# @Lakers: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, 30 to 50 posts on game days, 10 to 15 on off days. Nearly all original posts, nearly all with a clip, photo or graphic; quote posts of players and the NBA account; no replies; reposts frequent.
- Length and shape: short captions of 15 to 100 characters beside a clip ("Luka. Pull-up. Bucket."). Final-score graphics carry a single line ("FINAL. Lakers 118, Suns 109."). A few paragraph posts of 150 to 250 characters for injury reports, lineup notices and promotions.
- Mechanics: sentence case or ALL CAPS for stress ("GAME DAY", "FINAL"), terse fragments, full stops. Purple and yellow heart emoji, a fire or ball emoji on highlights; hashtags rare (#LakeShow). Player names in full or by first name for stars. Times written as "7:30 PM PT". @mentions for opponents and broadcasters.
- Openers and closers: opens with the player and the play ("Reaves from deep."), or "GAME DAY"; closes with a viewing note ("Tonight on KCAL.", "Tip at 7:30.") or the score.
- Subjects: highlights, records, final scores, injury and lineup notices, game-day and tip-off times, merchandise and tickets, legends and Lakers history, community events.
- Register: proud, warm, hype-filled but never boastful; loud on wins, quiet or silent on losses; speaks with "we" and "our"; no mention of critics.
- Never: sarcasm at players, trade chatter or rumours, attacks on rivals, negative takes, long essays.
- In the style of:
  1. Cole Brandt for three. Crowd on its feet. 💜💛
  2. GAME DAY. Lakers vs. Nuggets, 7:30 PM PT. Let's go.
  3. FINAL. Lakers 121, Hornets 107. Seventh straight.
