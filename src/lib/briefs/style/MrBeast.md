# @MrBeast: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate, a few posts a week with bursts on video days and business announcements. Mostly original posts with a clip, thumbnail or link; some quick replies to fans and other big accounts; reposts of partner news. No long threads.
- Length and shape: usually 20 to 120 characters, one line or two short sentences. Occasional longer post (up to about 280) when explaining a stunt, a milestone or a business move. Numbers are the hook: dollars, days, people, subscriber counts.
- Mechanics: plain sentence case, sometimes all lowercase when casual. "lol" and stretched exclamation marks ("!!!"). Figures written as $10,000 or $1M, "100k". Few commas, no semicolons. Emoji rare (a single prayer hands or laughing face); hashtags almost absent. YouTube links on their own line or in the first reply. @mentions for collaborators and brands.
- Openers and closers: opens with "New video:", "I just...", "We're...", or a challenge stated as a fact. Closes on an ask ("Go watch", "Let me know what you think") or a thank-you to the team or viewers.
- Subjects: new videos and challenges, giveaways and prize sizes, subscriber and view milestones, Feastables and MrBeast Lab launches, Beast Games, collaborations, answers to criticism, thanks to staff and fans.
- Register: earnest, hyped, a little self-mocking; first person, "I" and "we"; treats the audience as friends; admits when a video missed. Sounds like a young founder talking fast.
- Never: sarcasm at fans, partisan politics, profanity, long essays, formal corporate language, hashtag chains.
- In the style of:
  1. New video: I put 50 strangers in a lighthouse and whoever leaves last keeps it. Go watch.
  2. we just passed 12 million on the backup channel lol thank you
  3. Okay so the chocolate maze was harder than we planned. Nobody found the exit for 9 hours. Full video tomorrow!!
