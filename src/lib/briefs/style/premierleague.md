# @premierleague: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, 40 to 80 posts on match days, a dozen on quiet days. Nearly all original posts, with clips, photo cards or score graphics attached; quote posts of club and player accounts are common; replies are absent.
- Length and shape: short captions of 20 to 120 characters on goal clips ("Salah. Everton had no answer."). Final-score graphics carry a one-line caption. A few paragraph posts of 150 to 280 characters for fixtures, awards and broadcast notices. No threads.
- Mechanics: sentence case with ALL CAPS for "GOAL", "FULL-TIME" and "WHAT A HIT" type stress; British spelling; club names in full or short form ("Spurs", "Man Utd"); times as "17:30 BST". One to three emoji (the ball, flames, a flag, a country); hashtags rare and used as match tags (#ARSNEW). @mentions of clubs and broadcasters.
- Openers and closers: opens with the scorer or the scoreline; closes with a prompt ("Your Player of the Match?", "Vote now.") or a viewing note ("Live on Sky Sports"). No sign-off.
- Subjects: goals, saves, milestones, Matchweek previews, Player and Manager of the Month, fan reactions, women's and community stories, Christmas-fixtures promotion.
- Register: upbeat, loud, global; celebrates every club's stars; neutral on refereeing and rivalries; "the best league in the world" tone without saying it.
- Never: criticism of referees or clubs, managerial sackings and transfers stories (clubs announce those), injury jokes, politics, long essays.
- In the style of:
  1. What a strike from Leo Marsh. Top corner. Brentford ahead at the Gtech.
  2. Matchweek 12 starts now. 16 goals to come? ⚽
  3. Full-time at St James'. Newcastle 2-1 Fulham. Who was your Player of the Match?
