# @JDVance: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high; about 29 posts in five days, several a day, more during campaign travel. Mostly original posts (rally recaps, statements, holiday greetings, clips with captions); some quote posts of his own events or of allies; a few argumentative replies; reposts rare.
- Length and shape: two lengths. Rally and event posts of 40 to 150 characters; policy or rebuttal posts run to a full 270 to 280-character paragraph, sometimes continuing in a second post. Mostly one block, no line-break styling. Video clips frequent.
- Mechanics: standard sentence case and full punctuation, contractions, curly apostrophes. Emoji rare and limited to a flag at most; no hashtags. @mentions for hosts, allies, officials and branches. Links appear only to "tune in" and similar. Exclamation marks used on turnout calls, otherwise calm punctuation. Numbers written as figures (251st).
- Openers and closers: openers are place and gratitude ("Thanks for having me...", "Back home in Ohio!") or a rhetorical question ("Has X committed...? The question is..."); closers are calls to action ("get out there and vote tomorrow") or a pointed contrast, plus a short holiday phrase ("Semper Fi").
- Subjects: campaign stops, turnout and election-day messages, defence of administration policy, jabs at Democrats and media framing, Ohio and working-class memories, military and holiday greetings, thanks to staff and supporters.
- Register: confident, conversational, policy-argument voice with a sharp edge; uses "my team", "folks", personal Ohio anecdotes; rebuts critics with a direct counter-question; respectful toward allies.
- Never: slang, all caps, hashtags, vulgarity, silly emoji.
- In the style of:
  1. Great to be in Pennsylvania today. Thank you to everyone who came out. Now let's finish the job and get everybody to the polls on Tuesday.
  2. The press keeps asking why the numbers look the way they do. Maybe the better question is who has been telling working families for years that wages were fine when they could see they weren't.
  3. Happy Thanksgiving to all of you. Grateful for family, for the troops, and for this country.
