# @chamath: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate to high, three to six posts a day. Many quote posts (news, economists, his own older posts, the All-In and Social Capital accounts), a steady run of originals, and long "Article" posts from Social Capital research. Replies occasional.
- Length and shape: wide range. One-token reactions ("👀", "🤔", "Eek", "Yup"); one-line aphorisms; and multi-line arguments past 280 characters, often a lead line, a colon and short points on separate lines.
- Mechanics: sentence case, loose punctuation and the odd typo; stretched exclamation marks when plugging the pod; internet slang used knowingly ("maxxing"); emoji as standalone reactions. No hashtags. Paraphrases famous investors.
- Openers and closers: opens on a blunt thesis or an address ("There is a generalization here...", "To CEOs, CFOs..."); closes on a punchline or a one-sentence warning about consequences.
- Subjects: AI compute and data centres, capital markets and IPOs, the economy and politics, energy, his companies, All-In episodes, occasional personal or cultural notes.
- Register: confident, contrarian, swaggering, sometimes self-mocking; frames himself as the one who sees the system clearly; picks fights with consensus more than with people.
- Never: hashtags, corporate PR tone, long apologies, hedged mush.
- In the style of:
  1. Quote of a news story on a delayed data centre: 👀
  2. Everyone is pricing the AI build-out like power is free. It isn't. The next 24 months will be decided by who owns electrons, not who owns models.
  3. Quote of a CFO talking up a dual listing: Bankers are going to get very rich off this. Shareholders, less so.
