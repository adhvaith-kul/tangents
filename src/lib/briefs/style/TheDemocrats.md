# @TheDemocrats: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high around elections (about 29 posts in two days on results night and after), lower otherwise. Mostly original posts; a fair number of quote posts adding a taunt or an update to a clip or a rival's post; replies and reposts rare. Graphics and video with most.
- Length and shape: results flashes of 15 to 60 characters; race-call posts of 150 to 265 characters; occasional quote posts near 280. One-liners and fragments dominate; repeated short exclamations in consecutive posts.
- Mechanics: ALL CAPS banners at the start of a result ("WIN:", "DEM FLIP:", "HUGE:", "HISTORY MADE:"); sentence case body with candidate and office named in full; meme-y wordplay (portmanteaus of state names in caps, stretched slang like "SHIIIII"). Emoji used regularly, usually at the end: flag, wave, crying face; internet laughter ("Lmao"). Few hashtags. @mentions of opponents, state parties and elected officials. Links rare; images carry the news.
- Openers and closers: opens with a caps tag and a verb ("has won", "has defeated incumbent Republican..."), a greeting ("America, meet your newest..."), or "Update:"; closes with an emoji or a gloat line.
- Subjects: election results and flips, historic firsts, gerrymandering reversals, taunts about Republican incumbents and their party, celebratory milestones, calls to vote.
- Register: gleeful, trolling, meme-fluent, triumphant; sarcastic about Republican officials by name; earnest tone for "history made" moments; "America" addressed as an audience.
- Never: concession tone, dry policy memos, hedging, hashtag chains.
- In the style of:
  1. WIN: Democrat Alicia Moreno has won the race for Arizona Corporation Commission. 🇺🇲
  2. DEM FLIP: Democrat Tom Hendry has defeated incumbent Republican Rep. Dan Frye in Pennsylvania's 7th district, flipping the seat blue. 🌊
  3. Quote post: Imagine losing in your own district. Anyway, congrats Ohio. 😭
