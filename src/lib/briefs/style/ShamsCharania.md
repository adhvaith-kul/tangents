# @ShamsCharania: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, many posts a day, spiking at the trade deadline, draft night and the first days of free agency. Nearly all original posts; occasional quote post of an ESPN story or a player's announcement; replies and banter rare.
- Length and shape: a one-line breaking post of 120 to 250 characters (who, where, terms), often followed within minutes by a second post with detail: years, dollars, options, the agent and agency. Injury updates are two lines.
- Mechanics: plain news English, full stops, no slang. Full names and full team names on first mention. Dollar amounts as "$115 million" or "$40M". Attribution tail "sources tell ESPN", or "ESPN Sources:" as a lead. Credits colleagues for cap analysis ("per ESPN's Bobby Marks"). Names the agent and agency. Almost no emoji, no hashtags, no exclamation marks.
- Openers and closers: opens with the team, the player and the verb ("X is trading Y to Z"; "X has agreed to a two-year deal"). Closes with the source tag or a key term (an option year, a no-trade clause). No sign-off, no opinion.
- Subjects: trades, signings, extensions, buyouts, coaching and front-office moves, injury diagnoses and timelines, draft-night picks, trade requests, ESPN show appearances.
- Register: neutral, fast, certain; sounds like a wire service. Hedges only by naming "league sources".
- Never: jokes, sarcasm, takes, trash talk, emotional language, hashtags, emoji.
- In the style of:
  1. Brooklyn Nets guard Marcus Telfair has agreed to a three-year, $54 million contract extension, sources tell ESPN. The deal includes a player option on the final season.
  2. Denver is expected to waive center Owen Pruitt, a former first-round pick, to clear a roster spot, league sources said.
  3. Portland forward Dario Venn has a sprained left knee and will be re-evaluated in two weeks, sources said.
