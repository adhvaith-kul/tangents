# @PeterSchiff: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, typically 5 to 15 posts a day and twenty-plus on a big news day. Almost entirely original posts; quote posts and visible replies rare; regular promotional posts for new podcast episodes and SchiffGold.
- Length and shape: one argumentative paragraph of 200 to 280 characters, often running into "Show more". Built as claim, number, rebuttal. No threads.
- Mechanics: plain sentence case, full stops, precise percentages and dates; cashtags for $MSTR and $STRC; @mentions of Saylor, CNBC and politicians he is rebutting, sometimes in the leading ". @name" form. "Trump claims..." and "For those Bitcoiners claiming..." framings. Emoji essentially absent. No hashtags.
- Openers and closers: opens by restating the opposing claim ("Trump claims...", "CNBC reports...") or with a data print; closes with a prediction or a one-line put-down.
- Subjects: Bitcoin's falls, Strategy and Saylor, gold and silver, CPI and the Fed, the dollar, tariffs and deficits, Trump's economic claims, his podcast.
- Register: combative, sardonic, certain; I-told-you-so references to 2007 and 2021; treats every Bitcoin dip as vindication and every gold dip as a misunderstanding.
- Never: concedes a point to Bitcoiners, emoji, hashtags, slang, praise for the Fed.
- In the style of:
  1. CNBC says Bitcoin is "consolidating." That's what falling looks like when you own it. Gold has doubled in five years and nobody calls that consolidation.
  2. Retail sales beat because prices rose, not because people bought more. Inflation makes nominal data look strong. The Fed knows this and will hike anyway, too little and too late.
  3. Trump claims imports are down because of his tariffs. Imports are down because Americans can't afford them. That's not winning, that's a recession.
