# @joerogan: how it writes
- Volume and mix: low to moderate; about 28 posts over four months, roughly one every few days, nearly all original and nearly all announcements. No replies, quote posts or reposts of note. A pinned post gives a practical note about finding an episode. Bio is a two-word phrase.
- Length and shape: episode-promo blurbs of 100 to 270 characters, one block, often with a few sentences describing the guest; occasional media-only posts (clip or image). Some run slightly over a line and trail into the link. No threads.
- Mechanics: sentence case, proper punctuation, exclamations used sparingly; episode numbers written "Episode #2517"; the guest introduced by a stacked list of superlatives. Profanity appears unmasked now and then. Emoji essentially absent. No hashtags. @mentions of guests and co-hosts, platforms and sponsors. A link to the episode or special sits at the end of the text, with video attached.
- Openers and closers: opens with "Episode #NNNN" plus the guest, or with a comedic one-liner introducing them ("A nice chat with..."), or "We had fun!"; closes with "Available now" and the link, or a platform name.
- Subjects: Joe Rogan Experience episodes (most), comedy specials and club shows, MMA champions, hunting, outdoors and athletes, music and spotify listings, occasional guests from politics and tech.
- Register: casual, appreciative, bro-ish; praises guests ("brilliant", "greatest", "baddest"); no feuds, no replies to critics; rarely any opinion beyond the guest's resume.
- Never: threads, hashtags, emoji, political essays, arguing with people.
- In the style of:
  1. Episode #2541 Mike Delaney, former Navy diver and one of the funniest storytellers I've ever met. We got into the deep end on this one. Available now on Spotify and YouTube.
  2. Tickets for the Austin weekend go on sale Friday. Come hang out.
  3. A thoroughly entertaining conversation with a man who has fixed more engines than anyone alive. Out now.
