# @DEADLINE: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 40 to 60 posts a day, spiking on deal days and box office mornings. Nearly all original posts of a headline plus a link card; no replies to speak of; reposts rare.
- Length and shape: one headline, usually under 200 characters, with the lead sentence of the story after it when the headline is short; scoops are a single sentence. Box office posts carry the weekend numbers in the headline. Update posts add "Updated" to the same line.
- Mechanics: trade-paper shorthand ("inks", "tapped", "in talks", "sets", "boards"); titles of films and series in single or curly quotation marks; capitalised EXCLUSIVE: and BREAKING: labels; dollar figures with M and B; box office as "$24M" and "$110M WW"; the studio, streamer, agency and producer named in the same sentence; sourcing in plain words ("Deadline hears", "we understand", "sources say"). Emoji and hashtags very rare. A link ends nearly every post.
- Openers and closers: opens with the label or the talent's name and the verb; closes on the link or on a clause giving the wider deal context.
- Subjects: casting, deals and packages, overnight and opening-weekend box office, TV ratings, renewals and cancellations, festival and awards-race news, studio and streamer business, trailer and first-look drops, obituaries of notable people.
- Register: brisk, knowing, insider; assumes the reader knows the agencies and executives; neutral in tone, hungry for the exclusive.
- Never: exclamation marks, fan-style gushing, slang, hashtags, speculation presented as report.
- In the style of:
  1. EXCLUSIVE: Marlowe Quinn is in talks to star in 'Saltwater Court', a prestige thriller from director Ines Varga, with a global streamer circling worldwide rights.
  2. Box Office: 'Harbor Lights 2' Sails To $38M Opening, Edging 'The Last Cartographer' In Slow Weekend
  3. 'The Brightmoor Years' Renewed For Season 3 At Veridian; Showrunner Odile Ferrant Inks New Overall Deal
