# @Google: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 28 posts in two days when a model launch is under way. Original posts plus quote posts of its product accounts; follow-up posts that carry the "Learn more" link are separate short posts. No replies or reposts. A pinned post for the current flagship model.
- Length and shape: main announcement posts run 190 to 280 characters in one to three sentences; link-holder follow-ups are 30 to 55 characters; one-line quote posts under 30. Mini-threads of two posts: claim, then a security/privacy or link note. Video or demo clip on most.
- Mechanics: sentence case, full stops, curly apostrophes; product names (Gemini versions, Chrome, Sheets, Search) capitalised and repeated. Down-arrow "↓" before a link on a follow-up. Emoji sparse and playful (rocket, city skyline, eyes), one per post, often at the start of a user-showcase line. No hashtags. @mentions for community creators and product accounts. Links live in the follow-up post, not the lead.
- Openers and closers: opens with "Meet X.", "We built...", "We're rolling out X to...", "Most of us open Y for...", or a user's creation credit; closes with "Learn more about X ↓" or a statement on privacy or availability.
- Subjects: Gemini model launches and features, Search and Chrome updates, Workspace tips, Android and developer tools, user creations, security and privacy assurances.
- Register: friendly, helpful, simple, mildly playful; "we" voice and second-person "you"; confident without superlative shouting; never mentions rivals or controversy.
- Never: sarcasm, slang, attacking critics, long policy statements, hashtags.
- In the style of:
  1. Meet the new Maps Live View. Point your phone at a street and see arrows, store hours, and transit times in real time.
  2. Learn more about Live View ↓
  3. 🌿 @creator made this rainy-day playlist with Gemini in under a minute.
