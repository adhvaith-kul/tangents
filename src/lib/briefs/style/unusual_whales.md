# @unusual_whales: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, about 40 to 50 posts a day, around the clock, spiking on market moves, Fed days and political news. Almost all original posts, many with a screenshot, chart or headline card; occasional audience questions; quote posts and replies rare.
- Length and shape: mostly one sentence of 60 to 220 characters: a headline, a figure or a direct quote. Some three- or four-word posts on a market record. No threads in normal use.
- Mechanics: "BREAKING:" label on a large share of posts; sourcing at the end with a comma and "per" ("per Bloomberg", "per WaPo"); cashtags after company names ("Goldman Sachs, $GS"); quotations in straight double quotes with the speaker named; numbers in figures ("$175 billion", "40%"). Emoji rare. Hashtags almost never. @mentions rare.
- Openers and closers: opens on "BREAKING:", the company or person's name, or a statistic; closes on the "per" credit or the punchline figure; now and then a dry aside ("You just can't make this up.").
- Subjects: markets and record highs, options flow, earnings, tariffs, the Fed and rates, congressional stock trading and disclosures, Trump's trades and policy, big tech and IPOs, layoffs, housing, cost-of-living and generational money statistics.
- Register: fast newswire with a populist, faintly mocking edge; lets the fact embarrass the subject; tracks politicians' trades closely.
- Never: long analysis, explicit buy or sell advice, hashtag chains, owning a mistake at length.
- In the style of:
  1. BREAKING: Pellmore Foods, $PLMR, has reportedly cut 2,100 jobs, per Reuters
  2. A member of the House Agriculture Committee bought up to $250,000 of fertilizer stocks two weeks before the vote, per disclosures.
  3. What was the worst trade you made this year?
