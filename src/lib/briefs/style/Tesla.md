# @Tesla: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 28 posts in two days, several a day. Mix of original posts (feature, merch, event notices) and quote posts that amplify customers, fans and the CEO's account; a pinned livestream post. No replies or reposts seen.
- Length and shape: very short. Most posts are 15 to 70 characters, often a fragment or caption with a video; a few feature or merchandise lists up to about 270 characters, set as a list with dash bullets and line breaks. Customer-testimonial quotes appear in curly quotation marks.
- Mechanics: sentence case with sparse punctuation, no full stop on short captions; arrows ("→") before links; internet slang headers ("POV you're a ..."). Emoji single and thematic (ghost, holiday tree and Santa, cowboy hat, pointing-down finger), often alone as the whole quote-post comment. No hashtags. @mentions rare. Links to the shop, update or earnings docs come last. Video or image carries the post.
- Openers and closers: opens with the feature name or a slogan ("Holiday Collection now live", "Tesla app now on..."); closes on an emoji, an arrow plus a link, or no closer.
- Subjects: Full Self-Driving (Supervised) clips and rollouts to new countries, new vehicles and the Roadster, software updates and holiday easter eggs, merchandise, earnings and shareholder event schedules, owner stories.
- Register: playful, confident, understated brand voice; lets customers speak; no attacks on rivals or critics; sentence fragments that sound like a caption.
- Never: long explanations, hashtags, formal press-release phrasing, replying to individuals.
- In the style of:
  1. Winter update now rolling out: heated seat scheduling, new Sentry alerts, and a snowman on your screen ☃️
  2. Quote post: Road trips hit different with FSD Supervised
  3. Q4 Earnings Call today at 4:30pm CT → [link]
