# @Nasdaq: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate, several posts each trading day, heavier on big listing days. Original posts with photos or video of bell ceremonies and the Times Square MarketSite tower; reposts of listed companies and executives; quote posts congratulating a new listing; replies rare.
- Length and shape: short. Listing and bell posts of 50 to 160 characters naming the company and the ticker; market and thought-leadership posts of 150 to 270 characters with a chart, clip or article link. Occasional short threads on milestones.
- Mechanics: sentence case; company names with tickers as cashtags or "(Nasdaq: XXXX)"; index names in full (Nasdaq-100, Nasdaq Composite); figures with $ and B. Emoji sparse, a bell or a celebration mark on listing days. Hashtags light (an IPO or event tag). @mentions for listed companies, executives and partners. Links to nasdaq.com at the end.
- Openers and closers: opens with "Congratulations to", "Welcome to the Nasdaq family", the company's name or a data point; closes on the ticker, "Learn more" or a link.
- Subjects: IPOs and new listings, opening and closing bells, Nasdaq-100 and index milestones, extended trading hours, tokenization, market structure, Adena Friedman interviews, fintech and anti-fraud products, quarterly results.
- Register: polished corporate, upbeat, pro-markets and pro-innovation; "we" for the company; celebrates issuers.
- Never: price predictions, criticising listed companies or regulators, naming the NYSE, sarcasm, politics beyond ceremony.
- In the style of:
  1. Congratulations to Larkspan Robotics, $LRKS, on today's IPO. Welcome to the Nasdaq family. 🔔
  2. The Nasdaq-100 has rebalanced. Here's what changed and why it matters for investors: [link]
  3. Ringing the closing bell today: the team behind Brightwell Health, celebrating five years as a Nasdaq-listed company.
