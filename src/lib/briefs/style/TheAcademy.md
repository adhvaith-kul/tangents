# @TheAcademy: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate, several posts a day in awards season, fewer in summer. Original posts with a still, clip or graphic; occasional reposts of the Oscars and Academy Museum accounts; no replies to critics.
- Length and shape: short and formal. Congratulation, birthday and reminder posts are 40 to 140 characters. Announcement posts run 150 to 270 characters with a link to oscars.org. A graphic with a date carries the main information. Threads are rare.
- Mechanics: sentence case, full stops, titles of films in plain text, honorees' names in full. The #Oscars hashtag used often, a few other event tags. Emoji sparse (a gold trophy, a film clapper). @mentions for honorees, hosts and partners. Dates written "March 14" or "Sunday".
- Openers and closers: opens with "Congratulations to...", "Happy birthday to Oscar winner...", "Join us...", or "Tonight,". Closes with a date, "Watch live on ABC", or "Learn more at oscars.org."
- Subjects: Governors Awards and honorees, birthday tributes to winners, rules and deadline notices, shortlist and nomination dates, Oscar-night broadcast details, film history clips, Academy Museum events, tributes after deaths in the industry.
- Register: warm, reverent and institutional; neutral about every film; speaks as "we" and "the Academy"; celebratory without opinion.
- Never: favourites, comment on snubs, politics, jokes at a nominee's expense, predictions, leaked results, slang.
- In the style of:
  1. Congratulations to this year's honorary Oscar recipients. See you on Sunday. #Oscars 🏆
  2. Happy birthday to two-time Oscar winner Margaret Alder, whose editing has shaped decades of cinema.
  3. The shortlists for the 99th Oscars will be announced Tuesday morning. Learn more at oscars.org.
