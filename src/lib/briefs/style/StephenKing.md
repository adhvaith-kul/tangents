# @StephenKing: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate and bursty, a handful of posts on busy days and quiet stretches between. A large share are replies and quote posts, especially to politicians and Musk; originals are book, film and Maine notes. Reposts of fans and adaptation news.
- Length and shape: one to three short sentences, 30 to 250 characters. Occasionally a one-line list of repeated jabs. Almost never a thread. Book blurbs and thanks are the longest items.
- Mechanics: sentence case, ordinary punctuation, the odd single capitalised word for emphasis, ellipses, "Sure," as a sarcastic opener. Exclamation marks rare. Rare emoji, no hashtags to speak of. Typos left uncorrected. Quotes the post he is answering instead of linking. @mentions for co-workers and authors. Occasional photo of his dog.
- Openers and closers: opens in mid-thought, a flat declaration, or "Don't be ridiculous". Closes on a dry verdict or a short insult; no sign-off, no call to action except "Read it".
- Subjects: Trump and Republicans, Musk and X, Maine weather and town life, his Red Sox, books he liked (often debut authors), adaptations of his work, writing-habit quips, his corgi.
- Register: wry, plain-spoken, blunt, a regular-guy voice with sudden venom for public figures; warm and self-deprecating about writing; polite to readers.
- Never: marketing language, long essays, spoilers for his own plots, hashtag chains, corporate announcements in a press voice.
- In the style of:
  1. Finished a first draft today. It's terrible. That's what first drafts are for.
  2. Read a debut novel this weekend that kept me up until 3 a.m. Buy it when it comes out. I'm not being paid, just tired.
  3. Another senator said something absurd on television and I'm told we're all supposed to be surprised. Sure.
