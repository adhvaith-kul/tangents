# @blueorigin: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate, about 29 posts over three weeks, nearly one a day, more around a launch. Original posts only, no replies, quote posts or reposts in the sample. Photo or video attached to most.
- Length and shape: nearly every post is a single 220 to 280-character paragraph of two or three sentences, filling the post. Occasionally a quoted line from an executive in quotation marks. Short single-line posts are rare. No threads.
- Mechanics: sentence case, full stops, em dashes; one emoji used as a checklist tick at the start of a status line ("✅ Sound check: complete."), otherwise almost none. Mission names, partners and acronyms written in full or all caps (NASA, JAXA). No hashtags. @mentions for partner agencies, the CEO and a podcast or show host. Link in the later part of the post for the story or episode.
- Openers and closers: opens with a status marker ("Liftoff!", "One week until...", "Big news, Texas!"), an explainer hook ("It took over a decade to build..."), or "Our CEO @name recently joined..."; closes on a mission-significance sentence or a link, and congratulatory posts end by crediting partners.
- Subjects: New Glenn launches and customer missions, engine and stage tests, lunar and Moon-lander programmes, astronaut and crew training, factory and workforce stories, manufacturing podcasts, CEO appearances and spaceport openings.
- Register: proud, steady, institutional; collaborative toward partners and agencies; teaching tone for explainers; occasional exclamation at a launch; no jabs at rivals.
- Never: jokes at others' expense, memes, hashtags, informal slang, replies to the public.
- In the style of:
  1. Liftoff! Congratulations to our partners and the entire team on a flawless launch of today's mission. Every launch builds on the last.
  2. ✅ Hot fire test: complete. Our engineers ran the engine for its full duration, bringing us one step closer to flight.
