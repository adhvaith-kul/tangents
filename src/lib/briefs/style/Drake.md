# @Drake: how it writes
- Volume and mix: low and irregular; the feed is a run of release and promo posts with long gaps between them. Almost entirely original posts (one pinned, media only); no replies, and very few quote posts or reposts. Bio is a list of business partners and sites rather than a sentence.
- Length and shape: mostly titles and labels, 10 to 70 characters; the longest are a single sentence of about 120 characters. Never paragraphs, never threads.
- Mechanics: release titles in ALL CAPS or stylised caps ("OUT NOW", "AVAILABLE EVERYWHERE"), dollar signs replacing S in titles, otherwise title case; minimal punctuation. Emoji at the end as a mood string (dagger, owl, wilted flower, sun, feet), 1 to 3 at a time. Occasional hashtag on a show title. @mentions of collaborators, platforms and sponsors. A short-link to the release sits after the title.
- Openers and closers: opens with the project or song title, or "Congrats @name" / "Welcome to ..."; closes with the link, or "out now"/"available everywhere".
- Subjects: new albums, singles and deluxe editions (most), collaborators' records, streaming and TV projects he is involved in, sponsor and partner posts, tour or ticket giveaways, brand collaborations.
- Register: terse, cool and cryptic; confident; dry one-liners rather than explanation; thanks and congratulates collaborators briefly; never addresses critics.
- Never: long text, explanation of lyrics, political statements, arguments with rivals, replies to fans.
- In the style of:
  1. Midnight Roads out now  [link]
  2. Tour dates: NYC, Chicago, Toronto. Tickets Friday 🦉
  3. Congrats @collaborator on the album. Proud of you.
