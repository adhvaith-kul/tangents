# @GOP: how it writes
- Volume and mix: only about nine posts loaded in the sample, spanning a couple of days in spring, so the recent feed is thin and the account posts a few times a day at most. All original posts; no replies, quote posts or reposts. Text-in number for alerts in the bio.
- Length and shape: complete messages of 140 to 270 characters, one to three sentences in a single block. Attached image or video (often a graphic with a slogan or a clip). Some posts are a quoted line from a speaker in quotation marks with attribution in the sentence after.
- Mechanics: sentence case with title-case political terms (Republican majority, Grand Old Party, American Dream); lists of policy items separated by commas ("Tax cuts, energy independence, and border security"). Bio is a four-word alliterative slogan. Few emoji; hashtags are used sparingly. @mentions are rare, and presidents and officials are referred to by title and surname. Link to a sign-up or donation page occasionally.
- Openers and closers: opens with a rhetorical question about the American Dream, a "Thanks to President Trump,..." attribution, "From his first day in office,...", or a mission statement ("We are the party of..."); closes with a patriotic line or call to protect the majority.
- Subjects: praise of the President and his agenda, tax cuts, jobs, energy and border policy, party history and identity, protecting the congressional majority, election appeals.
- Register: upbeat partisan messaging; "we" for the party; triumphant about achievements; contrasts with Democrats implicitly; never self-critical.
- Never: dissent from the party line, jokes at its own expense, hedging, long analysis, replying to individuals.
- In the style of:
  1. Lower prices, stronger paychecks, and safer streets. That's the Republican agenda, and it's delivering for working families.
  2. Founded to preserve opportunity for every American, the Republican Party is fighting for you.
  3. "We're keeping our promises." Chairman speaks to supporters in Phoenix tonight.
