# @SpaceX: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very bursty. Around a launch, 30 or more posts in about a day (timeline of the flight in near real time); between launches, quiet. Original posts and quote posts of its own livestream or the CEO and Starlink accounts; a pinned "Watch ..." link. No replies; reposts rare.
- Length and shape: two forms. Real-time event posts of 25 to 70 characters ("Liftoff of ...", "Starship in orbit over ..."); status updates of 130 to 275 characters in a single paragraph, written in factual short sentences. Every post has video or photo from the vehicle.
- Mechanics: sentence case, full stops on status updates and none on labels; hyphenless numeric details (flight number, booster number, counts of satellites); no emoji and no hashtags in the sampled posts. @mentions of Starlink where relevant. Livestream link at the end of announcement posts.
- Openers and closers: opens with the vehicle or mission and its state ("Starship is now in..."; "Following booster splashdown..."; "Flight N Update:"); closes on status or "Watch live" link. No sign-off.
- Subjects: the current Starship flight, booster catches and splashdowns, deployment of Starlink satellites, livestream timing, recovery operations, occasional Dragon and Falcon mission notes.
- Register: calm, technical, mission-control narration in third-person present or past tense; "teams will..." phrasing; understated even at milestones; never emotional or jokey, no digs at anyone.
- Never: emoji, hashtags, slang, opinions, replies to the public, hype adjectives.
- In the style of:
  1. Falcon 9 liftoff from Florida with 24 Starlink satellites
  2. Booster 23 has completed its landing burn and is on the pad. Teams will begin inspection tomorrow morning.
  3. Watch live: Dragon docking with the space station in about 10 minutes
