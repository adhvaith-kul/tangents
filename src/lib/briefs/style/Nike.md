# @Nike: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate to high, about 27 posts in a week during a major tournament, otherwise lighter. Original posts mainly; a few quote posts of sub-accounts with a tiny caption; no replies or reposts. Photo or video on nearly every post.
- Length and shape: sports-copy captions of 50 to 130 characters, one or two clipped sentences; campaign or event teasers up to about 280 characters. Fragments are normal. One block, no threads.
- Mechanics: sentence case with full stops after each short sentence ("Always was, always will be."); no lowercasing. Wordplay lines built from the sport ("Time to elevate the game. Literally."). Emoji very rare. No hashtags. @mentions of athletes are constant and sit mid-sentence or at the sentence start; also sub-brand accounts. Links rare; media carries the post.
- Openers and closers: opens with a punchy declarative or a rhetorical pairing ("Not all returns heat up like this one."), or the athlete's name and achievement; closes on a mic-drop line or a pun, not a call to action.
- Subjects: tennis and football tournament results, individual athletes' wins and returns, product/campaign launches, culture events and collaborations, retirement tributes.
- Register: confident, athletic, poetic and sparing; celebrates athletes with admiration; dry puns; never mentions rival brands; no apologies.
- Never: hashtags, long explanations, exclamation marks in series, corporate hedging, replying to customers.
- In the style of:
  1. Back on court and nothing has changed. @athlete wins in straight sets.
  2. Fast feet. Faster minds. See what the new cleats can do.
  3. Seven titles. Zero days off. A career built one rep at a time.
