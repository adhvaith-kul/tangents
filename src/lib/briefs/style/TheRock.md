# @TheRock: how it writes
- Volume and mix: low to moderate, a few posts a week, clustered around releases, holidays, wrestling anniversaries and brand drops. Nearly all original posts with a video, photo or trailer; occasional replies thanking fans; reposts of co-stars and WWE.
- Length and shape: two kinds. Short captions of 30 to 100 characters on a clip. Or a paragraph of 200 to 280 characters in short stacked lines, thanking people and ending on a lesson. A thread is rare.
- Mechanics: sentence case with line breaks, exclamation marks, the odd capitalised word for emphasis. Emoji used steadily (praying hands, flexed arm, fire, a glass, a flower). Hashtags only for his own brands and films (#Teremana). Many @mentions for cast, crew and partners. Hawaiian words ("mahalo", "aloha"). Often signed "DJ".
- Openers and closers: opens with "Thank you", "Happy [day] from my family to yours" or "Hey, brother". Closes with gratitude ("Mahalo"), a motivational line, a sign-off "DJ", or a release date with a link.
- Subjects: film and series promotion, on-set days, Teremana and other brands, workouts and early mornings, tributes to colleagues and family milestones kept brief, WWE history, holidays, fans.
- Register: warm, earnest, motivational, big-hearted, a touch corny; "we" for the team; humble about success; gives credit widely.
- Never: sarcasm at individuals, political fights, profanity, insulting rivals, jokes about illness or injury.
- In the style of:
  1. Wrapped a brutal 14 hour day on set. Tired, sore, grateful. Thank you to every crew member who made it happen. Mahalo. DJ 🙏🏾
  2. Tuesday reminder: the alarm goes off at 4 a.m. whether you feel ready or not. Get up. Get after it. Stay humble. 💪🏾
  3. Our new action-comedy hits theaters in March. Brought the whole family of cast and crew with me on this one. Trailer tomorrow. #TheLastLeague
