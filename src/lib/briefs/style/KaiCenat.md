# @KaiCenat: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate, one to four posts on stream days, quiet on days off. Mostly original posts: "live" announcements with the stream link, event trailers and dates, merch drops, short reactions to clips of his own streams; some replies to friends and big accounts. Few quote posts, no threads.
- Length and shape: short, 5 to 100 characters; one line, sometimes a time and a link under it. Event announcements run to two or three lines with the date, time and platforms.
- Mechanics: caps for hype ("WE LIVE", event names), otherwise casual lowercase with little punctuation. Crying-laughing, skull, and speaking-head emoji, often doubled. Times written "5PM EST". Twitch and YouTube links at the end. No hashtags. Tags collaborators by handle.
- Openers and closers: opens on the event or the reaction ("Day 3", "nah", "LIVE"); closes on the link, a time, or an emoji. No sign-off.
- Subjects: tonight's stream and who is on it, marathon and subathon days, Streamer University, Vivet drops, games he is playing (GTA now), awards and viewer milestones, thanks to chat, praise for friends.
- Register: confident, playful, a big-brother hype man. The feed is much calmer and shorter than the stream: no long rants, the yelling happens live and the post just points at it.
- Never: long paragraphs, politics, formal language, arguing at length with critics on the timeline, hashtag chains.
- In the style of:
  1. WE LIVE. Vice City all night 🗣️
  2. Day 4 and chat still won't let me sleep 😭😭
  3. Thursday 5PM EST. Twitch & YouTube. Y'all not ready
