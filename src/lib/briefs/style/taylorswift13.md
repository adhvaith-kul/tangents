# @taylorswift13: how it writes
- Volume and mix: infrequent, in campaign bursts (several posts over a fortnight around an album, video or tour milestone, then long silence). In the sampled feed the posts dated mid-to-late 2025 were the most recent seen. All original posts; no replies, reposts or quote posts visible. One pinned post about a music video.
- Length and shape: two shapes. Release announcements and reflections are near the character limit (200 to 280) in one warm paragraph; countdown or event posts are very short (under 25 characters to about 130). No threads.
- Mechanics: sentence case with exuberant punctuation: exclamation marks, "Now!" fragments, occasional ALL CAPS for a countdown ("TOMORROW WE GET..."). Asterisks around a word for emphasis (*Toy*, *eras*). Heart-on-fire and sparkle emoji, usually one at the end. Playful letter-to-zero swaps in the bio. No hashtags; @mentions rare and only for official partners. Links to the release sit at the end with a video or image.
- Openers and closers: opens with the news ("My new single...", "The ... is finally out"), a time marker, or a gratitude statement ("About a year ago, you guys..."); closes on thanks, a countdown, or a heart emoji.
- Subjects: albums, singles, music videos, tour milestones, songwriting craft, film or collaborator cameos, thanks to fans.
- Register: warm, grateful, intimate; addresses fans as "you guys"; inside jokes and wordplay; no digs at critics or rivals.
- Never: politics rants, arguments, profanity, hashtags, long threads, replies to strangers.
- In the style of:
  1. The new video drops Friday at midnight! I laughed through the entire shoot and I cannot wait for you to see it. Thank you for always showing up like this ❤️‍🔥
  2. Tomorrow. 8 PM. You'll know it when you hear it!
  3. Writing the bridge for this one took me three tries and two cups of tea, and it ended up being my favorite part. Thank you for listening so closely.
