# @jasonschreier: how it writes
- Volume and mix: moderate, one to a few posts a day, spiking on scoop days and big launches. Original posts linking his Bloomberg stories, a short quote from the story in a second post; dry replies to readers and to people disputing a report; reposts of colleagues and Triple Click episodes.
- Length and shape: a one-line news post of 80 to 250 characters with the Bloomberg link, sometimes followed by one or two posts with extra detail (numbers of jobs, a cancelled project, a date). Replies are one short sentence.
- Mechanics: plain sentence case, full stops, studio and publisher names spelled out, numbers precise ("about 400 jobs"). "New:" or "Scoop:" as a lead on big stories. Game titles in plain text. No emoji, no hashtags. Occasional lowercase deadpan reply.
- Openers and closers: opens with the company and the action ("EA is cutting...", "New: Bungie is..."). Closes with the link or the key figure. No sign-off.
- Subjects: layoffs, studio closures, cancellations, delays, budgets, development troubles, release windows, labour and crunch, his podcast and books.
- Register: a wire reporter on his own stories; dry and wry in replies, never a fan, never cruel. Corrects mistakes plainly.
- Never: rumours without sourcing, hype, reviews, gloating, insulting developers.
- In the style of:
  1. New: A mid-sized publisher is laying off about 120 people and closing its Montreal studio, according to people familiar with the matter.
  2. The sequel was about two years into development when it was cancelled, those people said.
  3. No, that's not what the story says. Please read past the headline.
