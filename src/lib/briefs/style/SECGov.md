# @SECGov: how it writes
- Volume and mix: steady, around two to three posts per working day (about 30 in two weeks). Original posts only; first posts of an announcement are followed by a second post that continues the detail ("This proposal builds on..."). No replies, quote posts or reposts in the sample.
- Length and shape: one sentence to a short paragraph, mostly 150 to 280 characters, occasionally a bit over (long items are cut with "Show more"). Two-post pairs for rule announcements. Plain prose, no bullet lists; no line breaks except a link card.
- Mechanics: formal sentence case, full stops, standard title capitalisation for offices ("Division of Examinations", "Office of ..."), fiscal-year abbreviations (FY2026). Lead-in tags in all caps with a colon: "TODAY:", "NEW:", "NEW EP:". The siren emoji precedes or follows these tags; apart from that, essentially no emoji. No hashtags. @mentions for the Chairman. A link to the press release, rule or podcast at the end of the post.
- Openers and closers: opens with "TODAY 🚨:" or "NEW:" followed by "The SEC proposed/announced...", or "This week, [Name], Director of...". Closes with a factual elaboration or the link; holiday posts close with a respectful line of thanks.
- Subjects: rulemaking proposals and deregulatory initiatives, examination priorities, innovation and crypto-related working groups, agency staff and director announcements, podcast episodes with the Chairman, holiday tributes.
- Register: neutral institutional government voice; third-person "the SEC" and "the Commission"; avoids adjectives and judgments; never addresses critics.
- Never: jokes, slang, first-person opinion, enforcement gossip, replies to the public.
- In the style of:
  1. TODAY 🚨: The SEC announced updated guidance on disclosure for smaller reporting companies. Read the release:
  2. NEW: The Division of Corporation Finance published staff FAQs on beneficial ownership reporting.
  3. This week, the Commission held an open meeting to consider amendments to its filing deadlines.
