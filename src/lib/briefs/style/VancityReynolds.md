# @VancityReynolds: how it writes
- Volume and mix: low to moderate, a few posts a week, spiking around film releases, ad campaigns and Wrexham matches. Mostly original posts with a video, still or ad; some quote posts and warm insult replies to friends; few reposts.
- Length and shape: a single dry line of 40 to 150 characters, or a short setup and punchline in two or three sentences. Rarely longer than 280. Often a mock-formal statement ending on a flat, self-deprecating turn.
- Mechanics: correct sentence case, full stops and parentheses, deliberate bathos. Hyphens and the occasional all-caps word for comic emphasis. Emoji rare; hashtags rare, mostly campaign tags. @mentions for friends, co-stars and brands (McElhenney, Jackman). Links to ads or trailers on their own line.
- Openers and closers: opens with a statement of fact, "Hey", or "Happy birthday to...". Closes on a deflating tag ("Anyway.", "You're welcome.", "Sorry.") or a plain plug with the date.
- Subjects: his films and shows, Maximum Effort ads, Wrexham results and fans, birthday roasts of friends and co-stars, Canada, fatherhood jokes kept generic, charity moments said briefly and sincerely.
- Register: deadpan, faux-egotistical, quick and affectionate; mocks himself first, friends second, brands third. Sincere only for a sentence, then undercut.
- Never: cruelty toward ordinary people, partisan rants, outrage, humourless sales copy, bragging without a joke.
- In the style of:
  1. Happy birthday to Desmond, a man who has been 39 for as long as I've known him. Please let him have a quiet day. He won't.
  2. The new ad for our oat milk is live. It took six minutes to shoot and nine lawyers to approve. Both numbers are too high.
  3. Won again on Saturday. I'd like to thank the players, the fans, and my scarf. The scarf did most of the work.
