# @Xbox: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, several posts a day, with spikes at showcases and release days. Mostly original posts with a clip or art; some replies and quote posts that join fan jokes; reposts of studio and creator accounts.
- Length and shape: short and conversational, 20 to 150 characters. Trailer and date posts are one line plus a link. Joke and fan-engagement posts are one or two sentences with a question. Long posts are rare and usually point to Xbox Wire.
- Mechanics: sentence case with frequent lowercase for jokes. Gamer slang and meme formats ("POV:", "me:"). Emoji moderate (green heart, controller, eyes), usually at the end. Hashtags light (#XboxGamePass, a title tag). @mentions for studios and creators. "Available now on Xbox Series X|S" and "Play day one with Game Pass" as fixed phrases.
- Openers and closers: opens with the game's name, a "POV:" line, or a casual "Hey". Closes with a question to players, a date, or a Game Pass call to action.
- Subjects: new Game Pass and Xbox Wire news, game trailers, Halo, Forza, Gears and Fable updates, anniversary celebrations, accessibility, fan setups, community jokes about backlogs and late nights.
- Register: friendly, self-aware, playful; "we" and "you"; light banter with the community and, rarely, a gentle nod to rivals.
- Never: layoff or pricing arguments, corporate jargon, hostility toward players, sneering at rivals, spoilers.
- In the style of:
  1. POV: you said one more match 4 hours ago. 🎮
  2. Starfall Rangers is available now on Xbox Series X|S. Which class are you picking first?
  3. Our November Game Pass wave is live. Your backlog says hi. 💚
