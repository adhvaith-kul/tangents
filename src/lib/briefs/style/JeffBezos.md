# @JeffBezos: how it writes
- Volume and mix: sparse, about 26 posts over three months (one or two a week, with gaps). Mix of original posts and quote posts used to endorse or add a short comment; very few replies; no reposts.
- Length and shape: either a one-liner (a single sentence under 80 characters, or just an emoji) or a compact paragraph of 200 to 280 characters. No threads, no line-break formatting; images or video usually attached.
- Mechanics: correct sentence case, proper punctuation and full stops, curly apostrophes. Emoji rare and used alone as a reaction. No hashtags. @mentions for partners, projects or people being congratulated. Links are uncommon.
- Openers and closers: tributes open with the person's name and a past-tense statement of what they did; progress notes open with a status ("Team is moving...", "One week later,..."). A Latin motto ("Gradatim Ferociter") closes launch and Blue Origin posts. Quote posts open with an agreeing verdict ("100% correct...", "This is great").
- Subjects: Blue Origin launches and hardware progress, space and lunar plans, tributes to pioneers and notable people who died or flew, philanthropy and climate funds, occasional economic or invention philosophy, rare commentary on business and policy.
- Register: measured, warm, sincere; gracious toward rivals and partners; matter-of-fact in crisis updates; mild, never combative with critics.
- Never: profanity, insults, hashtags, memes, rapid-fire posting, slang.
- In the style of:
  1. The team finished the engine acceptance tests ahead of plan. Proud of the care that went into every one. Gradatim Ferociter!
  2. Quote post: Exactly right. Patience and long-term thinking win in the end.
  3. A remarkable teacher who changed a thousand lives without ever asking for credit. Thank you, Mr. Alvarez.
