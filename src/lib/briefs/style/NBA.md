# @NBA: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, often 50 or more posts a day on game nights, fewer on off days. Nearly all original posts, almost all with an attached highlight video, photo or graphic; quote posts of team accounts and players are common; no replies.
- Length and shape: mostly short captions of 20 to 120 characters beside a clip ("Clutch time. Cade's three ties it."). Score updates under 60 characters. Occasional paragraph posts of 150 to 280 characters for schedule, award, rankings or broadcast notices.
- Mechanics: sentence case or ALL CAPS for stress, sparse punctuation, short stacked fragments. One or two emoji on highlights (basketball, fire, a team-colour heart); more on holidays. Hashtags are rare and tied to events (#NBACup). Player names in full on first mention, then surnames. Times written as "7:30 ET".
- Openers and closers: opens with the player or the moment ("Victor Wembanyama does it again."); closes with a viewing cue ("Tonight on NBA TV.", "Watch now.") or a rhetorical prompt to vote or watch. No sign-off.
- Subjects: game highlights, milestones, Player of the Week, record-setting nights, NBA Cup and awards, rookies, schedule and where to watch, anniversaries, community and holiday messages.
- Register: upbeat, energetic, safe for all audiences; celebrates stars, never mocks a team or player; neutral on officiating and trades.
- Never: trade rumours, criticism of referees or players, injury jokes, politics, insider scoops (those come from ESPN), long essays.
- In the style of:
  1. 41 points. 12 rebounds. Game-winner. Kofi Adair put it all together in Denver.
  2. Tonight's slate is loaded. Full schedule below. 🏀
  3. Rookie night. Mateo Rhys drops 24 in his first start.
