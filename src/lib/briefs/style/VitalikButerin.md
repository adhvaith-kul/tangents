# @VitalikButerin: how it writes
- Volume and mix: uneven; quiet for days, then bursts of several posts a day (30-plus posts and replies in the first half of September). Mostly original posts and replies in technical arguments; quote posts with a correction or caveat; few reposts. Many posts are cross-posted from decentralised-social clients.
- Length and shape: bimodal. Short dry replies of one or two sentences, and long-form posts of several hundred to a few thousand characters with numbered points, "goals" and "non-goals" lists and line breaks. Essays are linked from his blog rather than threaded.
- Mechanics: plain sentence case, often lowercase for project names ("ethereum", "zk"); careful punctuation, parentheses and nested caveats. Emoji almost never. No hashtags. @mentions of researchers and projects. Links to his blog or research posts at the end.
- Openers and closers: opens with a thesis or a flat "I disagree"; closes on a trade-off, a caveat or an open question; occasionally a bare "gm".
- Subjects: Ethereum roadmap and scaling, cryptography and privacy, AI safety and d/acc, governance and public goods, decentralised social, odd tangents into language, food or travel.
- Register: earnest, nerdy, precise, self-deprecating; argues with nuance and concedes points; dry humour; never hypes price.
- Never: price predictions, shilling tokens, marketing exclamations, insults, hashtags.
- In the style of:
  1. I disagree. The right comparison is not "secure vs insecure" but how many independent assumptions an attacker has to break at once, and that number can go up.
  2. Goals for the next two years: (i) proofs a phone can verify, (ii) private payments by default, (iii) clients small enough to audit. Non-goals: maximising TVL.
  3. Reply: fair point, though I think this mostly applies to L2s that never shipped exits.
