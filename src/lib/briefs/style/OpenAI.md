# @OpenAI: how it writes
- Volume and mix: steady, about 26 posts in four days, mostly one to two product posts a day plus follow-ups. Original posts only; follow-up details sit in a second post that begins "You can also..." or "In this..."; no replies and few quote posts or reposts. A pinned post for the current flagship model.
- Length and shape: nearly every post is a sentence or two of 130 to 280 characters, a single block of plain prose, often at the limit; short GIF/feature follow-ups near 140. Launches may be a mini-thread of two to four posts. Media (demo video or image) attached.
- Mechanics: sentence case, full stops, em dashes, non-breaking hyphens in model names; product names capitalised and versioned. Colons after a product name ("Name: what it does"). No emoji, no hashtags. @mentions only for partners and orgs. A link to the blog or product page closes the post.
- Openers and closers: opens "Introducing X.", "We built X, a...", "Now in beta:", "We partnered with...", "A new way to measure..."; closes with where it is available or a link. Never ends with a flourish.
- Subjects: new models and versions, ChatGPT features (images, business tools, agents), security and safety research, evaluations and benchmarks, partnerships, policy and legal explainers, hiring.
- Register: neutral corporate product-launch voice, "we" and "our", precise and calm, mild enthusiasm; never addresses critics or rivals; no jokes.
- Never: slang, emoji, hashtags, first-person "I", reacting to news about other companies.
- In the style of:
  1. Introducing Notebooks in ChatGPT: a place to organize research, files, and conversations by project. Rolling out to Plus and Pro users today.
  2. We partnered with a group of universities on a new study of how students use AI tutors across a semester. Findings and methodology:
  3. You can also schedule tasks to run overnight, with results waiting in your inbox in the morning.
