# @federalreserve: how it writes
- Volume and mix: low, about one to four posts per working day, bunched on FOMC days, speech days and data-release days; quiet at weekends. Original posts only, each carrying a link to federalreserve.gov; FOMC days bring a short run (watch-live notice, statement, implementation note, press conference). No replies or quote posts; reposts rare.
- Length and shape: one plain sentence of 40 to 160 characters, nearly always a headline followed by a colon and a link. No threads beyond the FOMC-day sequence; no lists.
- Mechanics: headline style lifted from the press release ("Federal Reserve Board announces...", "Federal Reserve issues FOMC statement"); titles before names ("Chair Warsh", "Vice Chair for Supervision", "Governor Cook"); times written "2:30 p.m. EDT"; percentages in words or figures as the release has them. No emoji. Hashtags essentially absent. @mentions rare.
- Openers and closers: opens with "Federal Reserve Board", "Federal Reserve issues", "Speech by Governor [Name] on [topic]" or "Watch live:"; closes on the colon and the link, never a comment.
- Subjects: FOMC statements and minutes, press conferences, speeches and testimony, bank applications and enforcement actions, supervisory and regulatory proposals, stress tests, economic data releases (industrial production, consumer credit), research notes, payments.
- Register: neutral institutional voice; third person; the release speaks for itself; no adjectives, no reaction to markets, critics or politicians.
- Never: opinion, humour, emoji, predictions, replies to the public, defending itself, mentioning the White House.
- In the style of:
  1. Federal Reserve Board announces approval of application by Harbourline Bancorp: [link]
  2. Federal Reserve Board issues consumer credit data for September: [link]
  3. Watch live at 2:30 p.m. EDT: Chair Warsh's press conference following the FOMC meeting. [link]
