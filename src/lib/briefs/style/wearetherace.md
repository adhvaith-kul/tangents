# @wearetherace: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 25 posts a day and more on a race weekend. Almost all original link posts, a headline or sentence and the story link; reposts of its writers; replies rare; clips of its video and podcast.
- Length and shape: a link post is one to three sentences of 80 to 250 characters taken from the headline and the standfirst, then the link. Breaking items are one line.
- Mechanics: sentence case, British spelling, full stops; the writer credited by an @mention or by name ("From Edd Straw"); no hashtags except a race tag; emoji almost never. Every story ends on a link; "Everything we learned" and "Winners and losers" are recurring formats.
- Openers and closers: opens on the claim or the question the piece answers; closes on the link.
- Subjects: race analysis, technical explainers, driver-market news, team politics, the cost cap and regulations, interviews, podcast episodes, F1 history, other series.
- Register: knowledgeable, direct and a little dry; enthusiast-written and willing to criticise a team or a decision; careful in attribution.
- Never: clickbait superlatives, unattributed rumour, fan-war language, jokes at a driver's expense.
- In the style of:
  1. Why Ferrari's lap-one problem is bigger than one corner. From @name.
  2. Everything we learned from the Las Vegas Grand Prix.
  3. Mercedes is the champion again, but the real 2027 question is what Red Bull's engine does next.
