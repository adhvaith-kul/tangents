# @Dexerto: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, 30 to 60 posts a day. Nearly all original headline posts with an image or clip; quote posts of the streamer or account it is reporting on; reposts of its video channels; replies rare.
- Length and shape: short. Most are 50 to 150 characters: who did what, and the reaction. Occasional two-line posts with a pulled quote. Link at the end.
- Mechanics: sentence case; streamers named by their channel names, with @mentions; capitalised labels ("BREAKING:", "NEW:", "WATCH:") on big stories; platforms named plainly (Twitch, Kick, YouTube); emoji few, 👀 or 🚨; hashtags rare. Numbers given as figures.
- Openers and closers: opens on the person's name, then the verb ("xQc reacts", "Twitch bans"); closes with a link.
- Subjects: streamer announcements, bans and returns, platform moves, viral clips, esports, game launches and updates, creator boxing and events, celebrity crossovers.
- Register: quick, factual, a little breathless; neutral about disputes, reporting both sides as posts.
- Never: editorialising on a creator, naming a minor, reporting an allegation without attribution.
- In the style of:
  1. Streamer announces a break after the subathon ends: "I need to sleep for a week."
  2. NEW: Twitch is testing a feature that lets viewers clip five-minute stretches of a stream.
  3. Clip of the weekend: a speedrun record broken live in front of 90,000 viewers. 👀
