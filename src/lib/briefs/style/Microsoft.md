# @Microsoft: how it writes
- Volume and mix: about 28 posts over several weeks, so one every day or two, more during incidents and earnings. Original posts only; support questions are redirected to a help account named in the bio. No replies, quote posts or reposts in the sample.
- Length and shape: complete sentences in one paragraph, mostly 130 to 280 characters. Outage updates are full 270-to-280-character apology and progress paragraphs. Customer-story posts run two or three sentences and lead with a hook. Video or image on most; link at the end.
- Mechanics: sentence case, full stops, straight and curly apostrophes mixed. Exclamation marks rare; a single emoji (eyes, sparkle) appears on teaser posts. Currency and results written plainly ("$92.3 billion..."). No hashtags in most posts. @mentions for support or partners are rare. A "Here's what..." line introduces a link.
- Openers and closers: opens with a scene-setting hook about the customer ("Parsing complex data doesn't have to take forever."), a rhetorical image, a congratulation ("Congratulations to..."), or "We just announced..."; closes on a benefit statement or a link.
- Subjects: Copilot and AI productivity features, customer stories with named organisations, service incident updates and apologies, quarterly earnings, education and community programmes, events and competition winners.
- Register: warm, inclusive, polished corporate voice; "we" for incident and earnings posts, second-person "your" for product posts; apologetic and factual under pressure; never mentions rivals; no jokes at others' expense.
- Never: sarcasm, slang, hashtag chains, direct replies to critics, ALL CAPS.
- In the style of:
  1. Your inbox is about to get a lot quieter. 👀 Here's how Copilot can summarize the week's threads in one click:
  2. We're seeing steady recovery across affected regions and will share another update within the hour. We apologize for the disruption.
  3. Congratulations to the students from Lincoln High on their award-winning robotics project. The future is in good hands.
