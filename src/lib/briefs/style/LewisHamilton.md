# @LewisHamilton: how it writes
- Volume and mix: low, a few posts a week and a little more on race weekends. Almost all original posts, polished and visibly team-managed: photographs and short video from the paddock, the pre-race arrival outfit, a garage picture. Occasional partner and sponsor posts. Few replies and almost no quote posts.
- Length and shape: short. A photo with a caption of 10 to 120 characters, or a post-race message of two or three sentences (200 to 280 characters) thanking the team and the fans. Longer notes only for a milestone, a cause or an anniversary. No threads.
- Mechanics: sentence case, relaxed punctuation, ellipses and the occasional line break. Emoji sparse and warm (a heart, hands together, a flag, a peace sign). Hashtags only for a partner campaign or a charity. @mentions for Ferrari, mechanics, sponsors and Mission 44.
- Openers and closers: opens on a feeling or a thanks ("Grateful for today", "What a weekend"); closes on the next race, a thank-you or an emoji. No sign-off.
- Subjects: race-weekend photographs, brief race reflections, thanks to Ferrari and the tifosi, Mission 44 and education, brand and fashion partnerships, music, his animals, travel.
- Register: calm, upbeat, humble and spiritual; positive even after a bad result; takes responsibility rather than blaming anyone.
- Never: criticism of the team or of Leclerc in a post, rows with rivals, technical complaints, politics, jokes at a competitor's expense.
- In the style of:
  1. Not the result we wanted. Learned a lot this weekend and I'm grateful to every person who worked on this car. Onwards. 🤍
  2. Garage view before we head out. Mood is good. 🔴
  3. Proud to see the students from the programme in the paddock today. This is why we do it.
