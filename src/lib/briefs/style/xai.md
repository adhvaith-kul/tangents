# @xai: how it writes
- Volume and mix: steady, about 30 posts in a week, bunched around model releases. Mostly original posts plus quote posts of partner and platform accounts. No replies or reposts. (Note: when visited, x.com/xai redirected to a profile titled with a SpaceX-branded superintelligence name and handle @SpaceXSI; the bio was empty. The writing style is the same product-launch voice.)
- Length and shape: short. Launch announcements of 90 to 190 characters; platform-availability posts of 30 to 65 characters ("X is now on [cloud/platform]"); a benchmark claim of 120 to 150 characters. Mini-threads of two or three posts: headline then a capability line. Charts or demo clips attached.
- Mechanics: sentence case, short declarative sentences, full stops; model versions with decimal numbers (a point release is repeated in nearly every post). Superlatives stated flatly ("the world's most ... "). No emoji, no hashtags. @mentions for partners, platforms and integrators (cloud providers, code editors, router services). Links at end of the lead post.
- Openers and closers: opens "Introducing X.", "X is here.", "X is now on/available on [platform]", "Use X in Y", "We are glad to support..."; closes on a capability or discount note ("50% off until [date]"); no sign-off.
- Subjects: Grok model releases, voice and transcription models, agent bots, plan/pricing changes, availability on partner clouds, partner integrations and sponsored projects.
- Register: terse, confident product voice; benchmark-driven; collegial toward partners; never addresses critics, rivals or news.
- Never: jokes, emoji, hashtags, long explanations, opinions, replies.
- In the style of:
  1. Introducing Grok Imagine 2.0. Faster generation, longer clips, and better control over motion.
  2. Grok 4.8 is now available on [partner platform].
  3. Use Grok in Notion. Summarize pages, draft updates, and answer questions over your workspace.
