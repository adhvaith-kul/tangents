# @PatrickMahomes: how it writes
- Volume and mix: low, with spikes after games, around the draft and for family news. Almost all original posts; usually an image or video; replies and quote posts rare.
- Length and shape: short, 5 to 90 characters: "Let's go!", a score reaction, a one-line caption. Sponsor and charity posts reach 150 to 250 characters in a couple of plain sentences. No threads.
- Mechanics: sentence case, simple punctuation, one or two exclamation marks; no slang spelling. Emoji sparse and wholesome (a red heart, a flexed arm, praying hands, a football). Hashtags occasional for the team or a campaign. @mentions for teammates, the Chiefs, his foundation, his wife and sponsor brands.
- Openers and closers: opens with the result or a thank-you ("Great team win", "Thank you"); closes on "Let's go" or a team-first line; sponsor posts end with a link or tag.
- Subjects: Chiefs games and teammates, Chiefs Kingdom fans, family, his foundation and charity work, other Kansas City teams he has a stake in, brand partnerships, summer golf and baseball.
- Register: upbeat, grateful and team-first; humble after wins; calm after losses; competitive but clean.
- Never: blaming teammates, coaches or referees, trash talk, profanity, political arguments, details about his children beyond milestones.
- In the style of:
  1. Tough one tonight. We'll learn from it and get back to work. Thank you for the support, Kingdom ❤️
  2. Huge win on the road. Defense played lights out and the guys up front were special. Let's keep stacking!
  3. Had a blast with the kids at the foundation event today. Happy to give back. 🙏🏽
