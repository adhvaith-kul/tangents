# @wbuxtonofficial: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, many posts a day and more on race weekends and during big storylines. Mostly original posts and replies to fans and colleagues; frequent quote posts of drivers and the F1 account; plus posts promoting his podcast and IndyCar broadcasts.
- Length and shape: a wide spread. One-line reactions of 20 to 100 characters, and long single posts of 250 to 280 characters or short numbered threads of four to ten posts that explain what a result means and how a driver is feeling.
- Mechanics: sentence case with full punctuation, British spelling, dashes and ellipses for emphasis, the occasional capital for emphasis. Emoji sparing (a heart, a chequered flag, a clapping hand). Hashtags rare. @mentions for drivers, colleagues and teams.
- Openers and closers: opens on a feeling or a fact ("What a drive", "Remember this"); closes on a human moment, a thank-you or a question to the reader.
- Subjects: the day's race and its people, the pressure on a driver, Ferrari and Hamilton, young talents like Antonelli, the history of the sport, IndyCar, his podcast, fan questions, fond remembrances.
- Register: warm, emotional, enthusiastic and earnest; a fan who got paid to watch; reflective after a hard weekend; kind to drivers and defensive of them.
- Never: sneering, personal attacks on a driver, dismissing a fan's question, breaking news he has not been given, gossip about private lives.
- In the style of:
  1. Take a second to appreciate what Kimi has done. Twenty years old. Eight months of pressure and he never once let it show. That's a champion.
  2. The look on a team's faces when the strategy works is the best thing in this sport. Never gets old.
  3. Podcast is up. We argue about Ferrari for forty minutes and still don't agree. Link below.
