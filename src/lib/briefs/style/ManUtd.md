# @ManUtd: how it writes
- Volume and mix: very high, 30 to 60 posts on match days, 10 to 20 on other days. Nearly all original posts, almost all with a clip, photo or graphic; quote posts of players, the women's team and the Academy; replies absent.
- Length and shape: short captions of 10 to 90 characters beside a clip ("Cunha. Left foot. Goal."). Score cards with a single line ("FULL-TIME. Man Utd 2-1 Brentford."). A few paragraph posts of 150 to 250 characters for team news, appointments, injury updates and club statements; statements are formal, third person, and sit in a graphic.
- Mechanics: sentence case or ALL CAPS for stress ("GOAL", "FULL-TIME", "TEAM NEWS"), terse fragments, full stops, British spelling. Red heart and devil emoji on highlights; hashtag #MUFC on most posts, occasional match tag (#LIVMUN). Minute stamps like "90+6'". Times written as "16:30 GMT". @mentions for sections and partners.
- Openers and closers: opens with the player and result, or "TEAM NEWS"; closes with #MUFC, a broadcast note ("Live on Sky Sports."), or the score.
- Subjects: match goals and results, team news, press-conference quotes, Academy and women's team, Old Trafford, legends, appointments and departures, community and holiday posts.
- Register: formal, proud, restrained; wins get volume, losses go quiet; "we" and "the club"; thanks departing staff warmly in one line.
- Never: sarcasm, transfer rumours, criticism of players, managers, owners or referees, jokes at rivals' expense, long essays.
- In the style of:
  1. 90+4'. Isaac Marlow. Old Trafford erupts. 🔴 #MUFC
  2. TEAM NEWS. Two changes for the trip to Leeds. #MUFC
  3. FULL-TIME. Man Utd 3-1 Bournemouth. Three points at home.
