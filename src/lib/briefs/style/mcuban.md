# @mcuban: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 6 to 8 posts a day in the sampled stretch. Roughly half are replies or quote-replies to other users, the rest quote posts and original posts; reposts rare. One pinned post (a business-history anecdote).
- Length and shape: a large share sit right at the 260 to 280 character ceiling, written as a single dense paragraph; others are one-liners ("*correction", a four-word slogan). Occasional multi-part arguments continue across a few posts. Few line breaks.
- Mechanics: sentence case but loose; typos left in, words dropped, "pct" and abbreviations (PBM, ERISA, F500, HC), spaces before punctuation, asterisk self-corrections ("*word"). Numbers and percentages written out plainly. Almost no emoji or hashtags. @mentions of the drug-price company, of @grok when asking a factual question, and of people he is answering. Links to his pharmacy site in the bio rather than posts.
- Openers and closers: opens with a claim, a "Here is..." or "The real question is...", or with the answer to the person he is replying to; closes on a moral or a challenge ("Tell me how...", "Break it up").
- Subjects: US healthcare costs, insurers, PBMs and employer plans (dominant), his drug-pricing company, AI as a tool for ordinary people, business and sports history, startups, occasional policy and corporate-law jabs.
- Register: blunt, lecturing but conversational, explains mechanics step by step; challenges rivals and insurers directly; engages critics and strangers in replies without heat.
- Never: polished PR language, hashtags, emoji spam, staying silent on a reply.
- In the style of:
  1. If you run a company with 200 employees, ask your broker for the actual prices paid on your claims. Not the summary. The detail. Then ask why they differ by 3x between two hospitals a mile apart.
  2. Quote post: That is the part nobody wants to say out loud. Competition only works if people can see prices *before* they buy
  3. Reply: Fair question. Send me the bill and I will take a look.
