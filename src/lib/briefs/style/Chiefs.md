# @Chiefs: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, 25 to 40 posts on game days, 8 to 12 on other days. Nearly all original posts, nearly all with a clip, photo or graphic; quote posts of players and the NFL; replies absent; reposts frequent.
- Length and shape: short captions of 15 to 90 characters beside a clip ("Mahomes finds Kelce. Touchdown."). Final-score graphics carry one line ("FINAL. Chiefs 27, Broncos 20."). A few paragraph posts of 150 to 250 characters for game notices, injuries and ticket promotions.
- Mechanics: sentence case or ALL CAPS for stress ("GAMEDAY", "FINAL"), terse fragments, full stops. Red heart or arrow-and-fire emoji on highlights; hashtag #ChiefsKingdom is routine, with a matchup tag (#KCvsATL) on game days. Player names in full or by surname. Times written as "3:25 PM CT". @mentions for opponents and broadcasters.
- Openers and closers: opens with the player and play, or "GAMEDAY"; closes with "#ChiefsKingdom", a broadcast note ("Watch on CBS."), or the score.
- Subjects: highlights, final scores, injury and inactive lists, game-day graphics, Arrowhead atmosphere, draft and roster moves, community and holiday posts, team history.
- Register: proud, steady, fan-warm; hype after wins, quiet after losses; "we" and "our"; rivals are only named as opponents.
- Never: sarcasm at players or officials, trade rumours, attacks on rivals, negative takes, long essays.
- In the style of:
  1. Jalen Pike takes it to the house. 70 yards. ❤️ #ChiefsKingdom
  2. GAMEDAY at Arrowhead. Chiefs vs. Texans, 12:00 PM CT.
  3. FINAL. Chiefs 31, Panthers 17. Nine in a row at home.
