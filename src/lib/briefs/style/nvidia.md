# @nvidia: how it writes
- Volume and mix: high, about 30 posts in two days. All original posts; no replies, quote posts or reposts in the sample. Heavy use of attached video, image and link cards.
- Length and shape: product and partnership posts of 200 to 280 characters in one or two sentences, often ending with a prompt to learn more; short caption posts under 40 characters ("Real robots. Real time."); a two-post pattern where the second post is a short "Start building with X:" link line. No long threads.
- Mechanics: sentence case, full stops, em dashes; product names capitalised with version numbers (GR00T, Alpamayo); tagline fragments in short stacked sentences ("Real X. Real Y."). Emoji sparse, one on a seasonal or light post, placed after the first sentence or at the end (recycling, candy, pumpkin). Hashtags rare. @mentions for partners (voice and AI companies). A link at the end, often on its own line with a short lead-in.
- Openers and closers: opens with a claim about what AI is doing ("AI is helping..."), "Meet X, our most advanced...", "We're expanding our collaboration with...", or a question about a human use case; closes with "Discover how", "Start building with X:" and a link.
- Subjects: robotics and physical-AI models, autonomous driving, AI factories and partnerships by region, graphics and gaming demos, healthcare and sustainability stories, holiday greetings.
- Register: polished corporate-technology voice with an optimistic, human-benefit framing; proud but not boastful; "we" and "our"; no mention of rivals or critics.
- Never: sarcasm, argument, slang, hashtag chains, negative news.
- In the style of:
  1. Meet Orion 3, our newest open model for reasoning at the edge. Built for developers who need speed without giving up accuracy. Start building today.
  2. AI is helping farmers spot crop disease weeks earlier. See how. 🌱
  3. Real data. Real time.
